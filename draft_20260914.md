**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s Spring Surge: New CCP‑Resolution Drill, AI‑Focused Advisory Meeting, and a Wave of Enforcement Actions Targeting Fraudulent Funds and Misleading Advisers  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

On September 3, senior officials from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the Federal Reserve Board, and the Bank of England gathered for a tabletop exercise. The session examined how the two countries would coordinate a “central counterparty” (CCP) resolution – the process for winding down a clearing house that has become insolvent or otherwise unable to meet its obligations. The exercise highlighted gaps in cross‑border communication, data‑sharing protocols, and the legal steps needed to protect market participants when a CCP fails.  

In the same week the SEC announced several high‑profile enforcement actions.  

* **Ponzi‑Scheme Charges in New Jersey** – Ernest Ossei Boateng and two of his companies, Intercontinental Wealth Network LLC and I Wealth Network LP, were charged with raising roughly $16 million from more than 200 investors through false promises of high‑return, low‑risk investments.  

* **San Francisco Bay Area Private‑Fund Fraud** – Mark D. Hanf, former CEO of Pacific Private Money Group LLC, and Hoai‑Nam Chu Phan, former COO of a PPMG subsidiary, were charged with running a multimillion‑dollar offering fraud that misrepresented the use of investor capital and the performance of the fund.  

* **False Adviser Filings** – The SEC charged 38 entities for filing materially false Form ADV disclosures between 2025 and 2026. The false filings made the entities appear to be registered investment advisers, allowing them to attract retail investors under the guise of legitimacy.  

Separately, the SEC’s Investor Advisory Committee (IAC) will host a public meeting on September 10 to discuss the growing use of artificial‑intelligence (AI) technologies in public markets and the agency’s regulatory response. The IAC will hear from market participants, technology vendors, and consumer‑advocacy groups about AI‑driven trading, data‑analytics tools, and disclosure practices.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑border CCP coordination is now a compliance priority**  
Fintech firms that provide clearing, settlement, or post‑trade services must recognize that a CCP failure could cascade across jurisdictions. The joint U.S.–U.K. drill signals that regulators expect market participants to have contingency plans that align with both domestic and foreign resolution frameworks. For compliance teams, this means revisiting business‑continuity procedures, data‑retention policies, and contractual language with counterparties to ensure they can meet the reporting and disclosure expectations of multiple regulators in a crisis.  

**Enforcement focus on “light‑touch” fund structures is intensifying**  
The two fraud cases involving private‑fund managers illustrate the SEC’s willingness to pursue individuals and entities that operate outside the traditional registered‑adviser regime. Many fintech platforms host “private‑placement” offerings, tokenized funds, or digital‑asset vehicles that rely on exemptions from registration. The SEC’s actions demonstrate that misrepresentations about fund strategy, performance, or use of proceeds will trigger swift investigation, even when the entities are not formally registered advisers. Compliance officers must treat all offering documents, marketing materials, and investor communications with the same level of scrutiny applied to registered advisers.  

**False Form ADV filings expose a new avenue for retail‑investor scams**  
The 38‑entity case shows that the SEC is cracking down on firms that masquerade as legitimate advisers through inaccurate regulatory filings. Fintech platforms that aggregate or distribute investment products must verify the authenticity of any adviser’s Form ADV before allowing them to market to retail clients. Relying solely on a “registered” label without confirming the underlying disclosures can expose the platform to secondary liability and reputational damage.  

**AI in the markets is moving from novelty to regulatory focus**  
The upcoming IAC meeting underscores that AI is no longer a peripheral concern. The SEC is actively seeking input on how AI‑driven trading algorithms, predictive analytics, and automated disclosure tools affect market integrity and investor protection. Fintech firms that embed AI into pricing, execution, or compliance workflows should anticipate tighter guidance on model validation, bias testing, and transparent reporting. Early engagement with the SEC’s expectations can prevent future enforcement surprises.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Update CCP‑contingency plans**  
  - Map the data‑flows and reporting obligations that would be triggered by a CCP resolution in both the U.S. and the U.K.  
  - Incorporate cross‑border communication protocols with your clearing partners and legal counsel.  
  - Conduct a tabletop test of your own processes within the next 90 days to identify gaps.  

- **Treat every offering as if it were registered**  
  - Review all private‑placement, tokenized‑asset, and “exempt” fund documents for accurate performance and use‑of‑proceeds disclosures.  
  - Implement a checklist that verifies the credibility of fund managers, including background checks and verification of any exemption filings.  
  - Require senior compliance sign‑off on marketing language that promises returns or risk mitigation.  

- **Validate adviser filings before onboarding**  
  - Pull the latest Form ADV for any adviser or “registered” entity you intend to work with.  
  - Cross‑check the disclosed services, disciplinary history, and assets under management against the information you present to investors.  
  - Flag any discrepancies and demand corrective filings before allowing the adviser to market on your platform.  

- **Prepare for AI‑related regulatory expectations**  
  - Document the data sources, model assumptions, and testing procedures for any AI system that influences trade execution, pricing, or investor communication.  
  - Establish a governance board that reviews AI model performance quarterly and records any bias‑mitigation steps.  
  - Draft a brief internal policy on AI disclosures to investors, outlining what the algorithm does, its limitations, and the oversight mechanisms in place.  

- **Monitor SEC guidance and IAC outcomes**  
  - Assign a team member to track the September 10 IAC meeting minutes and any subsequent SEC releases on AI.  
  - Incorporate any new guidance into your risk‑assessment framework within 30 days of publication.  
  - Use the meeting’s public comments as a benchmark to gauge regulator sentiment toward your own AI initiatives.  

By acting now, compliance teams can reduce the risk of enforcement, protect investors, and position their firms to operate smoothly in an increasingly coordinated, technology‑driven regulatory landscape.
