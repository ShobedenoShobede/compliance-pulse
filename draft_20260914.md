**HEADLINE**  
SEC cracks down on fraud, prepares AI rules, and tests cross‑border clearing – three urgent take‑aways for fintech compliance teams  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) released a busy day of news that touches every corner of fintech compliance.  

First, senior regulators from the United States and the United Kingdom met on September 3 for a tabletop exercise on how to handle the failure of a central counterparty (CCP). The session, attended by the SEC, the Federal Reserve, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, and the Bank of England, focused on coordination, communication, and the legal steps needed to unwind a CCP that can no longer meet its obligations.  

Second, the SEC announced two high‑profile enforcement actions. In one case, Ernest Ossei Boateng and two New Jersey companies he controls were charged with running a $16 million Ponzi scheme that promised investors high returns from “wealth‑building” programs. In a separate case, former executives of Pacific Private Money Group LLC (PPMG) in the San Francisco Bay Area were charged with a multimillion‑dollar fraud that resembled a Ponzi structure, using a private‑fund offering to lure investors.  

Third, the agency disclosed that 38 entities misrepresented themselves as registered investment advisers by filing false information on Form ADV between 2025 and 2026. Those false filings were used to convince retail investors that the firms were legitimate, when in fact they were not.  

Finally, the SEC’s Investor Advisory Committee announced a public meeting on September 10 to discuss the growing use of artificial‑intelligence (AI) tools in the public markets. The committee will hear from industry participants about the benefits and risks of AI, and the SEC plans to use that input as it shapes future guidance and rulemaking.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement is intensifying.** The two Ponzi‑type cases and the 38‑entity advisory‑firm scheme show that the SEC is willing to pursue both high‑profile fraudsters and smaller, less‑visible operators. For fintech firms that run crowdfunding platforms, robo‑advisors, or any kind of investor‑facing service, the message is clear: any misstatement—whether in marketing material, offering documents, or regulator filings—can trigger a swift investigation and hefty penalties.  

**AI is moving onto the regulator’s agenda.** The upcoming Investor Advisory Committee meeting signals that the SEC will soon issue concrete expectations for firms that rely on AI for trading, underwriting, or client advice. Even if your company does not yet use AI, the discussion will likely affect data‑quality standards, model‑validation practices, and disclosure requirements that will cascade down to downstream service providers.  

**Cross‑border clearing could change your risk profile.** The joint US‑UK tabletop exercise on CCP resolution highlights that regulators are preparing for a scenario where a major clearing house fails. If your fintech platform settles trades through a CCP, or if you rely on a third‑party clearing service for crypto or tokenized assets, you may be asked to provide detailed contingency plans, liquidity buffers, or client‑protection mechanisms that differ from today’s rules.  

**False advisory filings are a red flag for the whole ecosystem.** The 38 entities that pretended to be registered advisers did so by manipulating a single form that the SEC uses to verify legitimacy. This underscores the importance of independent verification of any partner, vendor, or service provider that claims to be a registered adviser. Relying solely on self‑reported data can expose your firm to reputational and financial damage.  

**Investor protection remains the regulator’s top priority.** All five updates share a common thread: the SEC is focused on protecting retail investors from deception, whether that deception comes from fraudulent fundraisers, bogus advisers, or opaque AI‑driven trading models. Fintech compliance teams must therefore adopt a “investor‑first” mindset in every policy, from onboarding to ongoing monitoring.  

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit your onboarding and due‑diligence processes.** Verify that every partner, sponsor, or adviser you work with is listed on the SEC’s Investment Adviser Public Disclosure (IAPD) website or has a current Form ADV on file. If a counter‑party relies on self‑certified filings, request the original documents and cross‑check them against the SEC database.  

- **Strengthen monitoring of fundraising activities.** Review any token sales, crowdfunding campaigns, or private‑fund offerings for red flags such as unusually high promised returns, vague use‑of‑proceeds statements, or pressure‑selling tactics. Implement automated alerts for rapid inflows that exceed typical thresholds for your client base.  

- **Begin an AI risk‑assessment now.** Map all AI models that influence client decisions, pricing, or trade execution. Document the data sources, model assumptions, and validation steps. Prepare a brief summary of how you mitigate bias, ensure data integrity, and monitor model performance—materials that will be useful when the SEC releases its AI guidance.  

- **Develop a CCP‑failure contingency plan.** Identify which clearing houses your platform depends on, and assess the potential impact of a sudden shutdown. Draft a high‑level response that includes client communication templates, liquidity sourcing options, and a timeline for moving trades to an alternative clearing arrangement.
