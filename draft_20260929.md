**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s latest enforcement wave and a new “innovation exemption” signal tighter disclosure, compliance, and digital‑asset rules for fintech firms.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements today.  

1. **Zoe Financial — Conflict‑of‑Interest Disclosure Failure**  
   The SEC settled charges against Zoe Financial Inc., a New York‑based registered investment adviser, for not fully and fairly disclosing material conflicts of interest to clients and prospects. The firm agreed to pay a civil penalty and to revise its disclosure practices.  

2. **Market‑Statistics Update – IPO Surge**  
   The SEC’s Division of Economic and Risk Analysis published fresh data showing a sharp rise in the number of initial public offerings and the total proceeds raised in the United States. The report highlights that IPO activity is outpacing the previous year’s levels, driven in part by fintech and SPAC listings.  

3. **CMI Capital — Fraudulent Investment Scheme**  
   CMI Capital LLC and its founder, Michael D. Williams, were charged for a scheme that raised roughly $860,000 from at least 18 investors, many of whom were law‑enforcement personnel. The SEC alleges misrepresentations and misuse of investor funds.  

4. **OTC Link — Regulation SCI Violations**  
   Broker‑dealer OTC Link LLC was censured and hit with a $575,000 civil penalty for repeated failures to meet the SEC’s Regulation Systems Compliance and Integrity (SCI) requirements. The violations involved inadequate controls over its trading systems and data reporting.  

5. **Innovation Exemption for Tokenized NMS Stock**  
   The SEC issued an “innovation exemption” that allows on‑chain trading of certain tokenized stocks listed on the National Market System (NMS). The agency is also seeking public comment on the framework, signaling a willingness to accommodate blockchain‑based securities while maintaining investor protection.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Disclosure remains non‑negotiable.** Zoe Financial’s case underscores that even well‑established advisers cannot rely on generic language when describing conflicts. Fintech platforms that bundle advisory, robo‑advice, or wealth‑management services must audit their client‑facing materials to ensure every material conflict—whether fee‑based, affiliate, or technology‑related—is disclosed in clear, plain language. Failure to do so can trigger civil penalties and damage reputation.  

**IPO activity creates new compliance hotspots.** The surge in IPOs means more companies will be onboarding on digital capital‑raising platforms, and many will use fintech solutions for investor onboarding, secondary‑market trading, or data analytics. Compliance teams should expect heightened regulator focus on the accuracy of prospectus information, the handling of non‑public material information, and the proper segregation of “quiet periods” for insiders.  

**Fraud risk is expanding into niche markets.** The CMI Capital enforcement shows that fraudsters are targeting specialized investor groups, including law‑enforcement personnel, and using sophisticated pitch decks to appear legitimate. Fintech firms that facilitate crowdfunding, token sales, or alternative investment products must tighten Know‑Your‑Customer (KYC) and anti‑money‑laundering (AML) checks, especially when dealing with high‑net‑worth or institutional investors.  

**SCI compliance is no longer optional for broker‑dealers.** OTC Link’s penalty demonstrates that the SEC will enforce SCI requirements aggressively, even after years of warnings. Any fintech that operates a trading venue, market‑making engine, or connects to legacy broker‑dealers must ensure its technology controls—change management, system testing, and real‑time monitoring—meet the same standards the SEC expects of traditional broker‑dealers.  

**Tokenized stocks are entering the regulatory mainstream.** The innovation exemption is a clear signal that the SEC will allow on‑chain trading of NMS‑listed equities, but only under a framework that preserves market integrity. Fintech firms developing or supporting tokenized securities must be prepared to register the tokens, maintain audit trails, and provide the same disclosures and reporting as conventional shares. The request for comment also opens a window for firms to shape the final rules, but it means compliance teams must start building the required controls now.  

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit all client‑facing disclosures.** Conduct a rapid review of advisory, fee, and conflict language across websites, apps, and onboarding documents. Add any omitted material conflicts and ensure the language is plain, prominent, and signed by the client.  

- **Update IPO‑related controls.** If your platform supports IPO participation or secondary trading of newly listed securities, verify that you have procedures for prospectus accuracy, insider‑trading monitoring, and quiet‑period compliance. Train staff on the heightened regulator focus on IPO data integrity.  

- **Strengthen fraud‑prevention checks.** Enhance KYC/AML screening for high‑value investors and for any entity that markets alternative or tokenized assets. Implement red‑flag alerts for unusually rapid fund movements, mismatched investor profiles, or repeated use of personal connections (e.g., law‑enforcement contacts).  

- **Conduct an SCI readiness assessment.** Map your technology stack against the SEC’s SCI requirements—change management, system testing, data integrity, and real‑time reporting. Document gaps and prioritize remediation, especially for any broker‑dealer or market‑making functions you host.  

- **Begin compliance planning for tokenized NMS stock.** Review the exemption’s terms, determine whether the tokens you intend to issue or support fall within its scope, and start building the necessary registration, reporting, and audit‑trail capabilities. Submit thoughtful comments during the public‑comment period to influence the final rule and demonstrate proactive engagement.  

By taking these steps now, fintech compliance teams can reduce the risk of enforcement actions
