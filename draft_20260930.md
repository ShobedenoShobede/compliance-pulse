**Headline**  
SEC Cracks Down on Fraud‑Heavy Messaging Scams, Undisclosed Conflicts, and OTC Compliance Gaps – What Fintech Teams Must Fix Today  

---  

### What Happened  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission announced five separate enforcement actions on September 30, 2026.  

First, the SEC charged several overseas‑run entities with a $15 million fraud that used WhatsApp, Telegram and other chat apps to lure retail investors into “confidence‑building” schemes. The perpetrators promised high, guaranteed returns, then disappeared with the money.  

Second, Zoe Financial, a New York‑based registered investment adviser, settled charges for not fully disclosing material conflicts of interest to clients. The firm failed to tell investors that it was receiving undisclosed compensation from third‑party product providers, a breach of its fiduciary duty.  

Third, the SEC’s Division of Economic and Risk Analysis released new market statistics showing a sharp rise in IPO activity and total capital raised in the U.S. market during the past year.  

Fourth, the agency charged CMI Capital LLC and its founder, Michael D. Williams, for a scheme that raised about $860,000 from at least 18 investors, many of whom were law‑enforcement officers. The funds were allegedly misused for personal expenses.  

Finally, the SEC censured OTC Link LLC, a broker‑dealer, imposing a $575,000 civil penalty for repeated failures to meet Regulation SCI requirements. The firm’s systems for monitoring and reporting trade data were found to be unreliable, exposing the market to operational risk.  

---  

### Why It Matters  

Fintech platforms that enable digital communications, investment advice, or trade execution are now under a tighter microscope. The messaging‑app fraud case shows that regulators view the use of everyday chat tools as a vector for securities violations. If your service lets users share investment pitches or links through WhatsApp, Telegram, or similar channels, you could be implicated as a conduit for fraud unless you have robust monitoring and reporting controls.  

The Zoe Financial settlement underscores that any conflict of interest—whether it’s a hidden fee, a referral arrangement, or a proprietary product tie‑in—must be disclosed clearly and early. Fintech firms that bundle advisory services with technology solutions (e.g., robo‑advisors, API‑driven wealth platforms) must treat the same disclosure standards as traditional advisers. Failure to do so can result in civil penalties, reputational damage, and loss of registration.  

The OTC Link penalty highlights that compliance with Regulation SCI is no longer optional for firms that handle market data or order routing. Even if your fintech does not operate a broker‑dealer, you may rely on third‑party market‑data feeds or provide execution services. Weak system integrity can trigger enforcement, especially when the SEC is already flagging repeated failures.  

Together, these actions signal a broader regulatory trend: the SEC is expanding its focus from classic brokerage houses to the entire fintech ecosystem, including messaging, advisory tech, and data‑distribution services. Compliance teams must treat every customer‑facing channel, every fee structure, and every data‑feed as a potential enforcement point.  

---  

### What to Do  

- **Map all communication pathways.** Identify every way users can share investment ideas (e.g., in‑app chat, external messaging links, social‑media integrations). Deploy automated screening for prohibited solicitation language and set up rapid reporting to the compliance team.  

- **Audit conflict‑of‑interest disclosures.** Create a checklist of all compensation, referral, or partnership arrangements that could affect client outcomes. Ensure that any material fact is presented in plain language before a client signs an agreement or receives a recommendation.  

- **Review your market‑data and trade‑execution stack.** Verify that any system handling order flow, price quotes, or trade confirmations meets the core reliability and reporting standards of Regulation SCI. If you rely on a vendor, obtain written assurance of their compliance and conduct periodic testing.  

- **Strengthen AML/KYC for “investment confidence” schemes.** Implement transaction monitoring rules that flag unusually high returns promised through messaging apps, especially when the sender is overseas. Require additional verification before allowing fund transfers that exceed typical thresholds.  

- **Train staff on the new SEC market‑statistics trends.** Use the latest IPO data to update risk‑assessment models and product‑launch strategies. Understanding the surge in public offerings can help you calibrate onboarding processes for new issuers and avoid over‑exposure to volatile securities.  

By taking these steps now, fintech compliance officers can reduce the risk of enforcement, protect their customers, and keep their firms on the right side of the SEC’s expanding focus.
