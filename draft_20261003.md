**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils New Crypto‑Custody Rules, Cracks Down on Fraudsters, and Pushes Retail Access to Private Markets – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Address Today  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five major items on October 3, 2026 that reshape the compliance landscape for fintech firms that deal with crypto assets, private‑market investments, and retail fundraising.  

1. **Crypto‑Custody Proposal** – The SEC issued a draft rule package that creates a “tailored framework” for how registered investment advisers (RIAs) and regulated funds (including registered investment companies and business development companies) may custody crypto assets. The proposal clarifies which custodial arrangements satisfy the “safeguarding” requirements of the Investment Advisers Act and the Investment Company Act, and it introduces new reporting, risk‑management, and internal‑control expectations for crypto holdings.  

2. **Meyer Global Management Charge** – The agency charged Meyer Global Management LLC and its chief executive, Owen E.H. Meyer, with defrauding retail investors in private funds that claimed exposure to SpaceX and other pre‑IPO securities. The SEC alleges false representations about the funds’ size, performance, and the ability to access high‑growth private deals, resulting in material losses for investors.  

3. **Veteran‑Targeted Fraud Scheme** – Two individuals, Christopher Kenji Dinelli and Jacob David “Kobe” Frankel, were indicted for operating a fund called “Beyond” that raised more than $8.7 million from 35 investors—most of them veterans—through misleading promises of high returns and “patriotic” investment themes.  

4. **Retail‑Investor Expansion of Private Markets** – The SEC voted to propose amendments that would broaden retail participation in private‑market offerings. The changes aim to increase capital formation by allowing new regulated fund structures, relaxing certain disclosure thresholds, and encouraging “responsible retailization” of private‑equity, venture‑capital, and other illiquid assets.  

5. **WhatsApp‑Based Investment Scams** – The commission announced charges against multiple overseas‑linked entities that used WhatsApp, Telegram, and similar platforms to lure U.S. investors into fraudulent schemes. The scams collected at least $15 million by posing as legitimate investment opportunities and exploiting the trust built through personal messaging apps.  

Together, these actions signal a dual regulatory thrust: tighter oversight of crypto custody and private‑fund fraud, paired with a willingness to open private‑market investments to a broader retail audience—provided firms can demonstrate robust investor protection.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Immediate compliance pressure on crypto‑custody practices** – Fintech platforms that act as custodians, or that facilitate custodial services for RIAs and regulated funds, must now map their current processes against the SEC’s draft expectations. The proposal calls for documented risk‑assessment frameworks, segregation of client assets, real‑time reporting of holdings, and periodic independent audits. Failure to align could trigger enforcement actions similar to those seen in the Meyer Global and veteran‑fraud cases, where the SEC punished misrepresentation of asset safety and performance.  

**2. Heightened scrutiny of private‑fund disclosures** – The Meyer Global and “Beyond” cases illustrate that the SEC will aggressively pursue any fund that markets private‑equity or pre‑IPO opportunities without clear, verifiable disclosures. Fintech firms that host private‑fund marketplaces, provide fund‑administration services, or offer advisory tools must ensure that every offering includes accurate performance data, transparent fee structures, and documented investor qualifications. The new retail‑access proposal will expand the pool of potential investors, magnifying the risk of non‑compliant marketing.  

**3. New fraud vectors via messaging apps** – The WhatsApp‑based scams show that fraudsters are exploiting informal communication channels to bypass traditional due‑diligence safeguards. Fintech services that enable peer‑to‑peer introductions, social‑trading features, or messaging within their platforms could inadvertently become conduits for these scams. The SEC’s willingness to charge overseas operators indicates that U.S. firms may be held responsible for failing to detect and block illicit outreach that occurs on their networks.  

**4. Opportunity and risk in retailizing private markets** – The proposed amendments to expand retail participation are a double‑edged sword. On one hand, they create new product lines for fintechs—such as “regulated private‑market funds” that can be sold to non‑accredited investors. On the other, they raise the bar for compliance teams to prove that these products meet the SEC’s “responsible retailization” standards, which include suitability assessments, clear risk warnings, and limits on liquidity‑risk exposure.  

**5. Enforcement momentum** – All five items were announced on the same day, underscoring the SEC’s coordinated enforcement agenda. The agency is signaling that it will not tolerate lax controls in crypto, private‑fund marketing, or fraud‑prone communication channels, especially when retail investors are involved. Compliance officers must treat each of these developments as a priority, not an optional add‑on.  

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map existing crypto‑custody workflows to the draft rule** – Conduct a gap analysis of your custody policies, segregation practices, and reporting mechanisms. Identify any missing controls (e.g., independent audits, real‑time asset‑valuation feeds) and develop a remediation plan within 30 days.  

- **Upgrade private‑fund due‑diligence checklists** – Require documented proof of fund size, performance history, and investor qualification for every private‑market offering on your platform. Implement a “red‑flag” matrix for marketing language that could be construed as misleading (e.g., promises of guaranteed returns, claims of exclusive access).  

- **Implement messaging‑app monitoring** – If your platform includes chat, direct‑messaging, or social‑trading features, deploy automated screening for known fraud patterns (e.g., unsolicited investment pitches, requests for payment via non‑bank channels). Establish a rapid‑response team to investigate and block suspicious accounts.  

- **Develop a retail‑investor suitability framework** – Align product onboarding with the SEC’s forthcoming retail‑access rules. Create a questionnaire that captures investors’ financial situation, investment experience, and risk tolerance before allowing access to private‑market funds. Document the suitability analysis for each retail client.  

- **Train staff on new enforcement trends** – Conduct a focused training session for sales, product, and compliance teams that covers the Meyer Global, veteran‑fraud, and WhatsApp scam cases. Emphasize the importance of accurate disclosures, verification of fund credentials, and vigilance against informal outreach channels
