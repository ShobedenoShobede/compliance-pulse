**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils New Crypto‑Custody Rules, Cracks Down on Fund Fraud, and Pushes Retail Access to Private Markets – What FinTech Compliance Teams Must Address Today  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released a flurry of actions on October 4, 2026 that touch every corner of the fintech compliance landscape.  

1. **Crypto‑custody proposal** – The SEC issued a draft rule package that creates a “tailored framework” for how registered investment advisers (RIAs) and regulated funds (including registered investment companies and business development companies) may hold crypto assets. The proposal clarifies the standards for safeguarding digital tokens, defines the types of custodial arrangements that are permissible, and sets reporting, risk‑management, and internal‑control expectations.  

2. **Enforcement blitz** – Three separate enforcement actions were announced:  
   * **Meyer Global Management (MGM)** and its chief executive were charged with defrauding retail investors who bought interests in a private‑fund vehicle that claimed exposure to SpaceX and other pre‑IPO securities.  
   * **Two individuals** – Christopher Kenji Dinelli and Jacob David “Kobe” Frankel – were charged for running a fraud scheme that raised $8.7 million from 35 investors through a fund marketed to veterans.  
   * **Multiple overseas‑linked entities** were charged for “investment confidence” scams that used WhatsApp, Telegram, and other messaging platforms to lure U.S. investors, with losses estimated at $15 million or more.  

3. **Retail‑private‑market rule amendment** – The Commission also voted to propose amendments that would broaden retail investor participation in private‑market offerings. The changes aim to increase capital formation by expanding the types of regulated fund structures that can accept retail capital, while embedding safeguards to protect less‑sophisticated investors.  

Collectively, these moves signal the SEC’s dual focus on expanding innovation (crypto custody, retail access) and tightening the net around fraud that exploits new technology and vulnerable audiences.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Crypto‑custody rules will become compliance baselines**  
Fintech firms that act as advisers, fund managers, or custodians of digital assets must be ready to meet the SEC’s forthcoming standards. The proposal does more than reiterate existing anti‑money‑laundering (AML) expectations; it demands documented custody policies, segregation of client assets, third‑party risk assessments, and regular reporting of crypto holdings. Companies that have been operating with ad‑hoc or “best‑effort” crypto safekeeping practices will need to upgrade their controls, documentation, and audit trails now, before the rule becomes final.  

**2. Enforcement trends highlight new fraud vectors**  
The three enforcement actions illustrate how fraudsters are leveraging emerging platforms and narratives to attract retail money. The MGM case shows that pre‑IPO hype can be weaponized, especially when coupled with the allure of crypto‑linked returns. The veteran‑targeted scheme demonstrates that demographic‑specific messaging (e.g., “support our heroes”) remains a potent lure. Finally, the WhatsApp‑based scams underline the regulatory risk of using unregulated messaging apps for investor outreach. For compliance teams, this means a broader definition of “advertising channel” and a need to monitor communications that fall outside traditional email or website domains.  

**3. Expanding retail access raises fiduciary and disclosure duties**  
The proposed amendments to “responsible retailization” will let more retail investors buy into private‑market funds, but they also increase the fiduciary responsibilities of advisers and fund sponsors. The SEC is likely to require clearer risk disclosures, suitability assessments, and ongoing reporting to retail participants. Firms that have historically limited private‑market exposure to accredited or institutional investors must reassess product eligibility, onboarding questionnaires, and investor‑education programs to avoid violating the new rules once they are adopted.  

**4. Cross‑border and technology‑driven fraud intensifies regulatory scrutiny**  
The WhatsApp‑related charges reveal that the SEC is willing to pursue actors operating from overseas when they target U.S. investors. This expands the compliance perimeter: fintech platforms that enable cross‑border payments, messaging, or social‑trading features may be viewed as conduits for illicit activity. Companies must ensure that AML/KYC controls extend to any third‑party communication tools embedded in their products, and that they can trace the flow of funds back to the ultimate beneficial owners.  

**5. Timing and resource allocation**  
All five items are emerging simultaneously, creating a “regulatory sprint.” Compliance officers have limited bandwidth, but the risks are material: non‑compliance with crypto‑custody standards could trigger civil penalties; failure to detect fraud‑related communications could result in enforcement actions; and inadequate retail‑investor safeguards could lead to costly remediation. Prioritizing the most immediate exposure—namely, the crypto‑custody proposal and the fraud‑related enforcement trends—will help teams stay ahead of the curve.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map crypto‑custody gaps** – Conduct a rapid gap analysis of existing digital‑asset custody arrangements against the SEC’s draft criteria (segregation, third‑party vetting, reporting). Document deficiencies and develop a remediation plan with clear milestones.  

- **Expand fraud‑monitoring controls** – Update your transaction‑monitoring rules to flag payments linked to messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal) and to detect unusually high‑frequency, low‑value transfers that resemble “confidence‑scam” patterns. Train staff to recognize demographic‑targeted pitches (e.g., veteran or pre‑IPO narratives) and to escalate suspicious outreach.  

- **Re‑evaluate retail‑investor onboarding** – Review all private‑market fund products to determine whether they will be eligible for expanded retail participation. Strengthen suitability questionnaires, risk‑disclosure documents, and investor‑education materials to meet the anticipated “responsible retailization” standards.  

- **Enhance third‑party due diligence** – For any technology partners that provide messaging, payment, or custodial services, require updated AML/KYC certifications, data‑privacy attestations, and evidence of robust monitoring. Include contractual clauses that obligate partners to cooperate with SEC investigations.  

- **Establish a rule‑tracking dashboard** – Assign a point person to monitor the SEC’s rulemaking docket for the crypto‑custody proposal and the retail‑private‑market amendments. Capture key dates (public comment periods, expected finalization) and set internal review checkpoints so that policy updates can be rolled out promptly.
