**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out crypto‑custody rules, cracks down on private‑fund fraud and expands retail access – what fintech compliance teams must address today  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The SEC announced a **proposed rule package** that creates a specific framework for how registered investment advisers (RIAs) and regulated funds may **custody crypto assets**. The proposal clarifies which entities can act as custodians, sets capital and risk‑management standards, and requires detailed disclosures to investors. The agency says the rules are intended to bring the same level of investor protection that applies to traditional securities to the rapidly growing crypto‑investment space.  

In parallel, the SEC filed **enforcement actions** against several parties for fraud. Meyer Global Management LLC and its CEO, Owen E.H. Meyer, were charged with deceiving retail investors in private‑fund offerings that claimed to hold stakes in SpaceX and other pre‑IPO companies. The agency also charged Christopher Kenji Dinelli and Jacob David “Kobe” Frankel for a scheme that raised $8.7 million from 35 investors through a fund marketed to veterans. Additionally, multiple overseas‑based entities were accused of a coordinated “investment confidence” scam that used WhatsApp and other messaging platforms to solicit at least $15 million from U.S. investors.  

Finally, the SEC voted to **propose amendments** that would broaden the “retailization” of private markets. The changes aim to give qualified retail investors more choice in private‑fund investments, encourage innovative fund structures, and support capital formation while preserving investor safeguards.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Crypto‑custody rules will affect every fintech platform that holds or services digital assets for clients.** The SEC’s definition of an acceptable custodian, the capital‑adequacy thresholds, and the required reporting will likely force many existing service providers to re‑evaluate their custody arrangements, upgrade security controls, or partner with a qualified third‑party custodian. Failure to meet the new standards could trigger enforcement action or limit a firm’s ability to market crypto‑related products.  

**The fraud actions send a clear signal that the SEC is intensifying scrutiny of private‑fund offerings, especially those targeting retail investors.** Firms that market pre‑IPO or “alternative” investments must ensure that all marketing materials, performance claims, and disclosures are fully substantiated. The cases involving Meyer Global and the veteran‑focused fund illustrate that the SEC will look beyond formal registration status and focus on the substance of the offering and the actual flow of investor funds.  

**The retail‑access proposal expands the pool of potential investors but also raises compliance complexity.** As more private‑market products become available to qualified retail investors, fintech firms will need robust suitability assessments, clear disclosure of risks, and mechanisms to verify investor qualifications. The proposal’s emphasis on innovation means regulators expect new fund structures, yet they will not tolerate shortcuts that compromise investor protection.  

Together, these developments mean compliance teams must **simultaneously tighten crypto‑custody controls, reinforce anti‑fraud safeguards in private‑fund marketing, and prepare for a broader retail investor base in private markets**. The overlap of these areas—digital assets, private securities, and retail participation—creates a regulatory nexus that will be a focus of SEC examinations in the coming months.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review and update crypto‑custody policies.** Verify that any custodian you use meets the proposed capital, segregation, and reporting requirements; if not, begin qualifying an alternative custodian or adjust internal controls to bridge gaps.  
- **Conduct a fraud‑risk audit of all private‑fund marketing.** Confirm that every claim about pre‑IPO or alternative investments is backed by verifiable documentation, and that investor communications include full risk disclosures.  
- **Implement or strengthen suitability checks for retail investors.** Build a process to confirm that any “qualified” retail participant meets the income, net‑worth, or experience thresholds the SEC will likely codify.  
- **Enhance monitoring of communication channels.** Deploy tools to detect and flag solicitation activity on messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, etc.) that could be used for unregistered offers or “confidence” scams.  
- **Prepare for the upcoming rule‑making comment period.** Assign a point person to track SEC releases, draft concise comments on the crypto‑custody and retail‑access proposals, and engage industry groups to shape practical regulations.
