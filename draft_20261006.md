**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out new fraud‑awareness push, crypto‑custody rules, and private‑market reforms – what fintech compliance teams must address today  

---  

**WHAT HAPPENED**  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) joined a coalition of U.S. and overseas regulators to issue a joint investor bulletin for World Investor Week. The bulletin warns that fraud schemes are becoming more sophisticated, especially those that use digital channels and target vulnerable groups such as veterans and retail investors new to private‑market products.  

In parallel, the SEC released a formal proposal that would give registered investment advisers and regulated funds a clear, tailored framework for holding crypto assets. The draft rules would require firms to meet specific custody standards, disclose how they safeguard digital tokens, and adopt risk‑management practices that are consistent with existing securities rules.  

The agency also announced two high‑profile enforcement actions. First, Meyer Global Management LLC and its chief executive were charged with defrauding retail investors in private funds that claimed to own stakes in SpaceX and other pre‑IPO companies. Second, two individuals were accused of running a scheme that raised more than $8.7 million from 35 investors—most of whom were military veterans—by promising returns from a bogus fund called “Beyond.”  

Finally, the SEC voted to propose amendments aimed at “responsible retailization” of private markets. The changes would broaden the range of private‑fund structures that can be offered to qualified retail investors, while imposing new disclosure, suitability, and liquidity safeguards to protect those investors.  

---  

**WHY IT MATTERS**  

1. **Heightened fraud risk across the board** – The World Investor Week bulletin signals that regulators see a surge in scams that blend traditional securities fraud with digital‑platform tactics. Fintech firms that onboard retail customers, especially through mobile apps or social‑media channels, must expect closer scrutiny of their marketing, onboarding, and monitoring practices.  

2. **Crypto‑custody rules will soon be binding** – The proposed crypto‑custody framework moves the SEC from a “wait‑and‑see” stance to a concrete set of expectations. Firms that already hold digital assets for clients will need to prove they have robust controls, insurance, and transparent reporting. Even companies that only plan to add crypto services must begin building compliance infrastructure now, or risk costly retrofits later.  

3. **Enforcement focus on private‑fund mis‑representation** – The Meyer Global and veteran‑targeted cases underline that the SEC will pursue aggressive action against any private‑fund offering that misleads retail investors, especially when the product is marketed as a shortcut to “pre‑IPO” gains. Fintech platforms that facilitate private‑market investments must tighten vetting of fund managers, verify ownership claims, and ensure that all promotional material is fully substantiated.  

4. **Retail access to private markets will expand, but with safeguards** – The responsible‑retailization proposal means more qualified retail investors will be allowed to invest in private‑equity‑style funds. However, the SEC is pairing that access with stricter disclosure, suitability questionnaires, and liquidity‑risk warnings. Compliance teams will need to redesign investor‑qualification flows and upgrade reporting systems to capture the new data points the rules require.  

5. **Cross‑border coordination is increasing** – The joint fraud‑awareness bulletin shows regulators are working together globally. Fintech firms that operate in multiple jurisdictions must anticipate harmonized expectations around anti‑money‑laundering, know‑your‑customer, and fraud‑prevention standards, and be prepared to share information with foreign regulators when requested.  

---  

**WHAT TO DO**  

- **Review and tighten marketing controls** – Conduct an immediate audit of all investor‑facing content (emails, ads, in‑app messages). Remove any language that promises guaranteed returns, “pre‑IPO” access, or uses high‑pressure tactics. Document the review and keep the approved copy on file.  

- **Start building crypto‑custody compliance** – Map any existing crypto‑holding processes against the draft custody requirements. Identify gaps in segregation, insurance, and audit trails. If you do not yet hold crypto for clients, develop a roadmap that includes policy drafts, technology upgrades, and staff training before the final rule is adopted.  

- **Upgrade private‑fund onboarding checks** – Implement a checklist that verifies fund manager licensing, ownership of underlying assets, and the existence of third‑party audits. Require fund managers to provide a detailed prospectus and independent valuation for any pre‑IPO or illiquid holdings.  

- **Enhance veteran and other vulnerable‑investor safeguards** – Add a flag in your CRM for investors who are veterans or belong to other protected groups. Apply an extra layer of suitability questioning and provide a mandatory “cool‑off” period before finalizing any high‑risk investment.  

- **Prepare for expanded retail participation** – Update your investor‑qualification workflow to capture the new data points the responsible‑retailization proposal will demand (e.g., net worth, liquidity needs, risk tolerance). Ensure your reporting platform can generate the detailed disclosures the SEC will require for each private‑market offering.  

By acting now, compliance teams can turn these regulatory moves from a surprise risk into a competitive advantage—demonstrating to regulators, investors, and partners that the firm is proactive, transparent, and ready for the next wave of fintech innovation.
