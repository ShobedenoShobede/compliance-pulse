**HEADLINE**  
SEC Cracks Down on Cherry‑Picking, Boosts Crypto‑Custody Rules, and Launches New Outreach – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Address Today  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five significant items on October 7, 2026.  

1. **Final judgment against former Western Asset co‑CIO** – The SEC moved for a consent judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, the ex‑co‑chief investment officer of Western Asset Management. Leech is accused of “cherry‑picking” securities for personal gain, violating fiduciary duties and anti‑fraud rules. The agency seeks a permanent injunction, civil penalties, and an industry‑wide bar on future securities‑related work.  

2. **National compliance outreach seminar** – The SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program announced a virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers on November 19, 2026. The session will focus on practical steps for chief compliance officers (CCOs) to embed risk‑based controls, especially around emerging products and technology.  

3. **World Investor Week fraud‑awareness bulletin** – In coordination with U.S. and foreign regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin for World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights rising scams targeting retail investors, especially those involving crypto, pre‑IPO opportunities, and “green‑washing.” It urges regulators and industry participants to promote investor resilience.  

4. **Proposed crypto‑custody framework** – The SEC proposed new rules that would give registered investment advisers (RIAs) and regulated funds a clear path to custody crypto assets. The proposal outlines eligibility criteria, segregation requirements, third‑party custodian standards, and reporting obligations, aiming to bring crypto custody under the same regulatory umbrella as traditional securities.  

5. **Charges against Meyer Global Management** – The SEC charged private‑fund adviser Meyer Global Management LLC and its CEO, Owen E.H. Meyer, with defrauding retail investors in funds that held interests in SpaceX and other pre‑IPO securities. The allegations include misrepresentations about fund performance, undisclosed conflicts, and failure to register the offerings. The SEC seeks disgorgement, civil penalties, and an industry bar on future securities activities for the individuals involved.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement signals zero tolerance for self‑dealing** – The Western Asset judgment underscores that the SEC will pursue aggressive remedies when senior investment officers misuse client assets for personal profit. For fintech platforms that allow advisers or algorithmic managers to select securities on behalf of clients, the message is clear: any appearance of cherry‑picking or preferential treatment will trigger swift enforcement. Compliance teams must audit selection processes, ensure transparent allocation rules, and document any conflicts of interest.  

**Crypto custody is moving from gray to regulated** – The proposed crypto‑custody rules are the most concrete step the SEC has taken to bring digital assets into the traditional securities framework. Fintech firms that provide custodial services, crypto‑focused RIAs, or fund platforms that hold tokenized securities must prepare for new licensing, segregation, and reporting demands. Ignoring the proposal could leave firms exposed to enforcement or limit their ability to offer crypto products to regulated clients.  

**Investor‑protection focus expands to pre‑IPO and “space” deals** – The Meyer Global case highlights the SEC’s growing scrutiny of private‑fund structures that market pre‑IPO opportunities, especially those tied to high‑profile companies like SpaceX. The agency is targeting misrepresentations, inadequate disclosures, and unregistered offerings. Fintech platforms that facilitate secondary markets for private‑company shares or crowd‑source pre‑IPO investments must tighten due‑diligence and ensure all offerings are properly registered or qualify for an exemption.  

**Regulators are coordinating globally on fraud** – The joint World Investor Week bulletin shows that fraud prevention is becoming a coordinated, cross‑border effort. Scammers are exploiting the speed and anonymity of digital channels, and regulators expect industry participants to share best practices and educate investors. Fintech firms that operate internationally need to align their anti‑fraud controls with both U.S. and foreign guidance, especially around KYC, AML, and marketing disclosures.  

**Outreach provides a roadmap for compliance teams** – The upcoming SEC virtual seminar is more than a lecture; it is an opportunity to hear the agency’s expectations directly from senior SEC staff. Topics will likely include risk‑based testing, technology governance, and the new crypto‑custody proposals. Attending (or reviewing the recorded session) will give compliance officers concrete checkpoints to align internal policies with regulator priorities before formal enforcement actions occur.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review and harden securities selection processes**  
  - Conduct a gap analysis of any “discretionary” or algorithmic selection tools used by advisers or the platform.  
  - Document the criteria, controls, and oversight for each trade, ensuring no individual can favor specific securities for personal gain.  
  - Implement a real‑time monitoring dashboard that flags trades deviating from approved allocation models.  

- **Begin a crypto‑custody readiness assessment**  
  - Map all crypto assets held for clients, noting whether they are stored in‑house, with a third‑party custodian, or on a hybrid solution.  
  - Compare current practices against the key elements of the SEC proposal: segregation, custody‑provider registration, audit trails, and periodic reporting.  
  - Draft a remediation plan to address any shortfalls, including potential upgrades to custodial agreements or technology platforms.  

- **Strengthen pre‑IPO and private‑fund offering controls**  
  - Verify that every private‑placement or tokenized pre‑IPO offering on your platform has a clear registration status or a valid exemption.  
  - Enhance disclosure templates to include performance history, fee structures, and any material conflicts of interest.  
  - Institute a “red‑flag” checklist for high‑profile deals (e.g., space‑tech, biotech) that triggers senior‑level review before marketing.  

- **Integrate global fraud‑prevention best practices**  
  - Align your anti‑phishing, social‑engineering, and deep‑fake detection tools with the guidance outlined in the World Investor Week bulletin.  
  - Update investor education material to cover emerging scams, especially those targeting crypto and private‑company investments.  
  - Establish a cross‑border incident‑response protocol with your legal and compliance teams to ensure rapid coordination with foreign regulators when needed.  

- **Plan participation in the SEC compliance seminar
