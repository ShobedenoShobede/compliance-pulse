**SECURITY ALERT: Cherry‑Picking Penalties, Crypto‑Custody Rules, and Pre‑IPO Fraud Charges Signal Tightening Grip – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track Today**

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items on October 8, 2026 that together reshape the compliance landscape for investment advisers, fund managers, and fintech platforms that touch securities.

1. **Final judgment against a former co‑CIO** – The SEC moved for a consent‑based final judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, the former co‑chief investment officer of Western Asset Management. Leech is accused of “cherry‑picking” – selectively allocating profitable trades to certain client accounts while leaving other accounts with poorer performance. The agency says the conduct violated the fiduciary duty owed to all clients and the SEC will seek civil penalties and an industry‑wide bar from future advisory roles.

2. **National compliance‑outreach seminar** – The SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program announced a virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers on **November 19, 2026**. The event will focus on practical steps for chief compliance officers (CCOs) to meet evolving regulatory expectations, including risk‑based testing, data‑analytics tools, and the new crypto‑custody proposal.

3. **World Investor Week fraud‑awareness bulletin** – In coordination with U.S. and foreign regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin for World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights rising scams targeting retail investors, especially around “pre‑IPO” opportunities and digital‑asset investments, and urges firms to strengthen investor‑education and detection mechanisms.

4. **Proposed rule for crypto‑asset custody** – The SEC unveiled a draft framework that would allow registered investment advisers (RIAs) and regulated funds to hold crypto assets directly under federal securities law. The proposal clarifies who may act as a qualified custodian, sets minimum safeguards (e.g., multi‑signature controls, audit trails, segregation of client assets), and requires periodic reporting to the SEC.

5. **Charges against Meyer Global Management** – The agency charged private‑fund adviser Meyer Global Management LLC and its CEO, Owen E.H. Meyer, with defrauding retail investors. The alleged scheme involved private‑fund interests in SpaceX and other pre‑IPO securities that were misrepresented as low‑risk, high‑return investments. The SEC claims Meyer concealed material risks, inflated valuations, and failed to disclose conflicts of interest.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement is widening beyond traditional misconduct.**  
The cherry‑picking judgment shows the SEC is willing to pursue senior portfolio‑management staff for selective trade allocation, even when the underlying securities are not illegal. For fintech firms that provide algorithmic trade‑execution or portfolio‑rebalancing services, the ruling underscores the need for transparent, equitable trade‑allocation policies and robust audit trails that can demonstrate consistent treatment of all client accounts.

**Crypto‑custody is moving from gray‑area to regulated reality.**  
The proposed rule signals that the SEC expects RIAs and funds to treat crypto assets with the same rigor as traditional securities. Fintech platforms that offer custodial services, token‑gateways, or integrated crypto‑wallets must be ready to meet custodial standards that include independent audits, segregation of assets, and real‑time reporting. Failure to align with the forthcoming rule could result in enforcement action or loss of registration eligibility.

**Investor‑fraud focus is intensifying, especially around pre‑IPO deals.**  
The World Investor Week bulletin and the Meyer Global charges together highlight a surge in scams that promise early access to high‑growth private companies. Fintech marketplaces that list private‑placement opportunities, or that enable “crowd‑investing” in pre‑IPO securities, will face heightened scrutiny. Regulators expect firms to conduct thorough due‑diligence, disclose all material risks, and verify that investors understand the illiquid, speculative nature of these products.

**The SEC is actively engaging the compliance community.**  
The upcoming virtual seminar is more than a training session; it is an opportunity for the SEC to signal its enforcement priorities and to gather feedback on proposed rules. Attendance will give compliance officers early insight into how the agency will interpret the crypto‑custody proposal, the expectations for trade‑allocation monitoring, and the evidentiary standards for fraud detection.

**Cross‑border coordination means broader expectations.**  
The joint fraud‑awareness effort with global regulators means that best‑practice standards are converging across jurisdictions. Fintech firms that operate internationally must harmonize their AML/KYC, investor‑qualification, and disclosure processes to satisfy both U.S. and foreign supervisory expectations.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit trade‑allocation logic.**  
  Review any algorithmic or discretionary trade‑allocation processes to confirm they treat all client accounts fairly. Document the criteria, run back‑tests for bias, and retain logs that can be produced in an SEC inquiry.

- **Prepare for the crypto‑custody rule.**  
  1. Map current crypto‑holding practices against the draft custodial safeguards (multi‑sig controls, segregation, audit frequency).  
  2. Identify gaps and develop a remediation plan—consider partnering with a qualified custodian that meets the proposed standards.  
  3. Update client agreements to reflect new custody disclosures and reporting obligations.

- **Strengthen pre‑IPO offering controls.**  
  Implement a checklist for any private‑placement or pre‑IPO product: verify issuer disclosures, confirm valuation methodology, disclose liquidity constraints, and obtain signed investor‑qualification statements. Train sales and product teams on the red flags highlighted in the fraud‑awareness bulletin.
