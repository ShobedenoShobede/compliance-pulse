**Headline**  
SEC Cracks Down on Cherry‑Picking, Boosts Crypto‑Custody Rules, and Targets Fraud in Private‑Fund Pre‑IPO Deals – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Act On Now  

---  

### What Happened  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five major items today that touch virtually every fintech firm that offers investment advice, manages assets, or provides custody services.  

1. **Cherry‑Picking Enforcement** – The SEC moved for a final judgment, by consent, against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. Leech is accused of “cherry‑picking” – steering client trades to benefit a personal account while disadvantaging the adviser’s funds. The agency filed a complaint that alleges violations of the Investment Advisers Act, including breach of fiduciary duty and fraudulent conduct.  

2. **National Compliance Outreach Seminar** – The SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program announced a virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers on **19 November 2026**. The session will focus on practical steps for chief compliance officers to strengthen supervisory systems, address emerging risks, and prepare for upcoming rule changes.  

3. **World Investor Week Fraud Awareness** – In coordination with U.S. and international regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin for World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights rising fraud schemes, especially those exploiting digital‑asset platforms and “pre‑IPO” investment opportunities, and urges investors to stay vigilant.  

4. **Proposed Crypto‑Custody Rules** – The agency proposed a new regulatory framework that would allow registered investment advisers and regulated funds to custody crypto assets directly under federal securities law. The proposal includes definitions of “crypto assets,” custody standards, record‑keeping, and reporting requirements, aiming to bring clarity to a market that has operated largely under a patchwork of state and self‑regulatory rules.  

5. **Charges Against Meyer Global Management** – The SEC charged private‑fund adviser Meyer Global Management LLC and its CEO, Owen E.H. Meyer, with defrauding retail investors in funds that held interests in SpaceX and other pre‑IPO securities. The complaint alleges misrepresentations about the funds’ performance, undisclosed fees, and failure to register the offerings, violating the Investment Company Act and antifraud provisions.  

---  

### Why It Matters  

These actions signal three converging priorities for fintech compliance: **fiduciary enforcement, crypto‑custody clarity, and heightened fraud scrutiny**, especially around emerging investment products.  

1. **Enforcement of Fiduciary Duties Is Intensifying** – The Western Asset case shows the SEC is willing to pursue senior investment professionals for even subtle breaches of duty, such as cherry‑picking. For fintech firms that rely on algorithmic trade routing, third‑party execution, or hybrid advisory models, any practice that could be interpreted as favoring a related party over clients can trigger enforcement. The consent judgment also sends a message that the SEC will seek final judgments quickly, leaving little room for prolonged litigation.  

2. **Crypto‑Custody Rules Will Soon Be Mandatory** – The proposed ruleset moves crypto custody from a regulatory gray area into the same compliance regime that governs traditional securities. Fintech platforms that already offer crypto wallets, custodial services, or advisory on digital assets must prepare to meet the same standards as custodians of stocks and bonds—robust internal controls, segregation of client assets, periodic audits, and detailed reporting to the SEC. Failure to adapt could result in enforcement actions or the loss of registration.  

3. **Fraud Risks Around Pre‑IPO and Private‑Fund Offerings Are Under the Spotlight** – The Meyer Global charges, coupled with the World Investor Week bulletin, underscore that the SEC is focusing on retail exposure to private‑market deals, especially those marketed through fintech portals. Misleading performance claims, undisclosed fees, and inadequate disclosures are now being treated as serious violations. Firms that facilitate “early‑stage” investments must reassess their marketing materials, suitability screens, and investor‑qualification processes.  

4. **Regulators Are Offering Guidance, Not Just Enforcement** – The upcoming November 19 seminar is a clear invitation for compliance officers to engage directly with the SEC, ask questions, and get clarification on supervisory expectations. Participation can help firms anticipate how the agency will interpret the new crypto‑custody proposal and the enforcement trends seen in the cherry‑picking and pre‑IPO cases.  

5. **Cross‑Border Coordination Means Global Consistency Is Expected** – The joint investor bulletin reflects a coordinated effort among U.S. and foreign regulators to combat fraud. Fintech firms operating internationally must align their anti‑fraud programs with both SEC expectations and the standards of counterpart regulators (e.g., FCA, ESMA, MAS). Inconsistent policies across jurisdictions could become a focal point for supervisory examinations.  

---  

### What to Do  

- **Review Trade‑Routing and Execution Policies**  
  - Audit any internal or third‑party routing logic to ensure client orders are not being steered to benefit related accounts.  
  - Document the rationale for routing decisions and retain records for the statutory period.  

- **Begin Gap Analysis for the Crypto‑Custody Proposal**  
  - Map current crypto‑custody practices against the draft standards (asset segregation, audit frequency, reporting).  
  - Identify required technology upgrades, custodial agreements, and policy rewrites.  

- **Strengthen Pre‑IPO and Private‑Fund Offering Controls**  
  - Verify that all marketing materials contain clear, balanced risk disclosures and fee breakdowns.  
  - Implement a robust suitability questionnaire for retail investors seeking access to private‑market deals.  

- **Register for the SEC’s November 19 Compliance Outreach Seminar**  
  - Assign a senior compliance officer to attend and prepare a list of questions related to crypto custody, supervisory systems, and enforcement trends.  
  - Capture the session’s takeaways and circulate a concise briefing to relevant business units.  

- **Align Anti‑Fraud Programs with Global Regulator Guidance**  
  - Update fraud‑detection analytics to flag unusual activity in digital‑asset and pre‑IPO transactions.  
  - Conduct a cross‑border policy review to ensure consistency with the joint investor bulletin’s recommendations.  

By acting now, fintech compliance teams can turn today’s regulatory headlines into a roadmap for stronger controls, reduced enforcement risk, and smoother integration of crypto assets into their service offerings.
