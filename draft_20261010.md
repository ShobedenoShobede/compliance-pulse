**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils Sweeping Proposals – Expanded Cross‑Trading, New Crypto‑Custody Rules, and a Nationwide Compliance Seminar – All Set to Redefine FinTech Risk Controls

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements today that together signal a broad push to tighten oversight of investment firms and to give clearer guidance on emerging technologies.

1. **Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the list of securities that a registered fund may trade with its affiliates, provided the transaction meets the existing “fair‑price” and “best‑interest” safeguards. The agency is seeking public comment on the draft language and expects a final rule later this year.

2. **Enforcement Action – Western Asset** – The Commission moved for a final judgment, by consent, against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The judgment stems from a “cherry‑picking” scheme in which Leech allegedly steered profitable securities to the firm’s own accounts while denying the same opportunities to client funds. The settlement includes a permanent bar from serving as an investment adviser and a monetary penalty.

3. **National Compliance Outreach Seminar** – A virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers is scheduled for November 19. The event, part of the SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program, will walk chief compliance officers through recent rulemaking, enforcement trends, and best practices for risk monitoring.

4. **World Investor Week Fraud Bulletin** – In coordination with global regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin aimed at heightening fraud awareness during World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights common scams, the rise of deep‑fake impersonations, and steps investors can take to verify communications.

5. **Crypto‑Custody Framework Proposal** – Perhaps the most consequential for fintech firms, the SEC unveiled a draft rulebook that would create a tailored custody regime for crypto assets held by registered investment advisers, registered investment companies, and other regulated funds. The proposal sets out licensing, segregation, and reporting requirements designed to bring crypto custody under the same federal securities standards that apply to traditional assets.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑Trading Flexibility Meets New Risk Controls**  
For fintech platforms that operate both as fund managers and as service providers to affiliated entities, the expanded cross‑trading list could open new trading opportunities and improve liquidity. However, the same flexibility comes with heightened scrutiny. The SEC’s emphasis on “fair‑price” and “best‑interest” testing means compliance teams must upgrade their pricing models and documentation to prove that every affiliate trade meets the same standards as a third‑party transaction. Failure to do so could trigger enforcement actions similar to the Western Asset case, where a breach of fiduciary duty led to a permanent industry ban.

**Enforcement Signals Zero Tolerance for Insider‑Benefit Schemes**  
The judgment against Ken Leech underscores that the SEC will continue to pursue “cherry‑picking” and other self‑dealing conduct, even when the misconduct appears limited to a single individual. Fintech firms that allow employees or algorithmic agents to route trades must ensure robust segregation of duties, real‑time monitoring, and an audit trail that can demonstrate impartial execution. The settlement also serves as a reminder that personal misconduct can quickly spill over to the firm’s reputation and regulatory standing.

**Seminar Offers a One‑Stop Compliance Refresh**  
The November 19 virtual seminar is not just an educational event; it is a strategic touchpoint for compliance officers to hear directly from regulators about how the SEC expects firms to implement the new cross‑trading and crypto‑custody rules. Attending (or reviewing the recorded session) will help teams prioritize remediation projects, align internal policies with the SEC’s enforcement focus, and avoid costly guesswork.

**Fraud Awareness Is No Longer a Seasonal Issue**  
The joint fraud bulletin released for World Investor Week reflects a growing, coordinated effort among regulators to combat sophisticated scams that target both retail and institutional investors. Fintech platforms that provide onboarding, KYC, or transaction services must integrate the bulletin’s warning signs—such as deep‑fake voice calls and spoofed email domains—into their fraud‑detection workflows. Ignoring these signals could expose firms to AML violations and reputational damage.

**Crypto Custody Rules Bridge the Regulatory Gap**  
Until now, many fintech firms have treated crypto assets as a “gray area,” applying internal best practices without a clear federal framework. The SEC’s proposed custody regime changes that calculus. Firms that already hold crypto for clients will need to obtain the required licenses, implement segregation of digital assets, and adopt the reporting formats the SEC outlines. Those that have avoided crypto custody may need to reassess their product roadmaps, as the new rules could make it easier for regulated competitors to enter the market with a compliant offering.

- **Review and Update Cross‑Trading Policies**  
  - Map every security type your firm trades with affiliates.  
  - Verify that pricing models meet the “fair‑price” test for each class.  
  - Document the decision‑making process and retain evidence for regulator review.

- **Strengthen Insider‑Dealing Controls**  
  - Implement real‑time trade monitoring that flags any deviation from market prices.  
  - Separate trade‑execution authority from portfolio‑management functions.  
  - Conduct a focused audit of recent trades to ensure no “cherry‑picking” patterns exist.

- **Register for the November 19 Seminar (or secure the recording)**  
  - Assign a senior compliance officer to attend.
