**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s 2026 agenda tightens cross‑trading, cracks down on cherry‑picking, rolls out a crypto‑custody framework, and launches two outreach pushes – everything fintech compliance teams need to know now.  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items today.  

1. **Cross‑trading rule expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act that would broaden the list of securities that a registered fund may trade with its affiliates. The change is meant to give funds more flexibility while preserving safeguards against conflicts of interest.  

2. **Final judgment against a former adviser** – The agency moved for a consent judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, the former co‑chief investment officer of Western Asset Management. Leech is accused of “cherry‑picking” – directing profitable trades to the firm’s own accounts while leaving less‑favorable trades to client funds. The judgment seeks civil penalties and bars on future securities activities.  

3. **National compliance outreach seminar** – The SEC announced a virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers on November 19. The session will focus on practical steps chief compliance officers can take to meet the agency’s expectations, especially around risk monitoring and record‑keeping.  

4. **World Investor Week fraud‑awareness bulletin** – In partnership with U.S. and international regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin urging the public to stay vigilant against fraud. The bulletin highlights common scams and provides tips for verifying investment offers.  

5. **Crypto‑custody rule proposal** – The SEC unveiled a draft rule that would create a specific framework for how registered investment advisers and regulated funds may hold crypto assets. The proposal clarifies custody responsibilities, security standards, and reporting requirements, aiming to bring crypto‑related activities under the same regulatory umbrella as traditional securities.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑trading changes affect every fund that trades with related parties.** Fintech platforms that enable fund‑to‑affiliate transactions must now review the expanded list of eligible securities. While the SEC says the goal is to increase efficiency, the underlying conflict‑of‑interest safeguards remain. Failure to apply the new limits could trigger enforcement actions similar to the cherry‑picking case.  

**The Western Asset judgment sends a clear warning.** Cherry‑picking is not a new concept, but the SEC’s decision to pursue a consent judgment against a senior executive underscores that the agency will hold individuals personally accountable. Fintech firms that provide trade‑execution services, algorithmic routing, or any “best‑execution” function must ensure that trade allocation logic can be demonstrated as fair and that audit trails are complete.  

**The November 19 seminar is an opportunity, not a distraction.** The SEC’s outreach program is designed to give compliance officers a roadmap for meeting expectations. Topics will likely include the new cross‑trading proposals, the crypto‑custody framework, and best practices for fraud prevention. Attending (or reviewing the recorded material) will help teams stay ahead of enforcement trends without having to chase every press release.  

**Fraud‑awareness messaging raises the bar for client education.** The joint World Investor Week bulletin means regulators expect firms to do more than post a disclaimer. Fintech apps that onboard retail investors must now embed clear, up‑to‑date fraud warnings, provide easy ways for users to verify solicitations, and train support staff to recognize scam patterns. Ignoring this could be viewed as a lapse in investor protection duties.  

**The crypto‑custody proposal could reshape how digital assets are held.** Until now, many advisers have relied on “general” custody rules that were not tailored to the technical realities of blockchain. The SEC’s draft introduces specific security controls, segregation requirements, and reporting formats. Fintech providers that offer custodial services, wallet integrations, or crypto‑focused advisory must begin mapping their current practices to the proposed standards. Early alignment can reduce the risk of costly retrofits once the rule is finalized.  

Overall, the five items paint a picture of an SEC that is tightening oversight across traditional and digital assets, while also offering guidance through seminars and public bulletins. For fintech compliance teams, the message is simple: review internal policies, upgrade controls where needed, and engage with the regulator’s outreach resources.  

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit cross‑trading processes** – Compare your current list of securities used in affiliate trades against the SEC’s proposed expanded list. Document any gaps and update trade‑allocation policies to reflect the new limits.  

- **Strengthen trade‑allocation controls** – Implement or verify existing automated checks that ensure trades are allocated fairly between the fund and any related parties. Keep detailed logs that can be produced quickly in an enforcement inquiry.  

- **Register for the November 19 compliance seminar** – Assign a senior compliance officer to attend (or watch the recording). Capture any guidance on the cross‑trading rule, crypto custody, and fraud prevention, and circulate key takeaways to the broader team.  

- **Upgrade investor‑education content** – Add the latest fraud‑warning language from the World Investor Week bulletin to onboarding screens, email communications, and help‑center articles. Train customer‑service reps to recognize the scams highlighted in the bulletin.  

- **Map your crypto‑custody workflow to the draft rule** – List all custody points (wallets, third‑party custodians, internal controls) and assess whether they meet the security, segregation, and reporting standards outlined in the proposal. Prioritize any gaps that could affect regulatory approval or client trust.  

By acting on these steps now, fintech firms can reduce compliance risk, demonstrate proactive stewardship to regulators, and position themselves for smoother operations as the SEC’s proposals move toward final rulemaking.
