**HEADLINE**  
SEC Rolls Out Five Major Moves – Expanded Cross‑Trading, Crypto Custody Rules, Enforcement Action, Outreach Seminar, and Global Fraud‑Awareness Push  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items today that touch on core compliance responsibilities for investment companies, advisers, and fintech platforms that serve them.  

1. **Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The change would broaden the list of securities that a registered fund may trade with its affiliates, provided the transaction meets existing fairness and disclosure standards.  

2. **Enforcement – “Cherry‑Picking” Judgment** – The Commission moved for a final judgment, by consent, against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The judgment resolves a long‑running “cherry‑picking” case in which Leech allegedly directed fund trades that benefitted him personally.  

3. **National Compliance Outreach Seminar** – The SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program announced a virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers on **Nov 19, 2026**. The session will focus on practical steps for chief compliance officers to meet the agency’s evolving expectations.  

4. **World Investor Week Fraud‑Awareness Bulletin** – In partnership with U.S. and overseas regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor‑education bulletin for World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights common fraud schemes and urges investors to stay vigilant as market conditions shift.  

5. **Crypto‑Custody Framework Proposal** – The agency proposed a new rule package that creates a tailored, securities‑law‑based framework for how registered investment advisers and regulated funds may hold crypto assets. The proposal clarifies custody, safeguarding, and reporting requirements for digital‑asset holdings.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑Trading Flexibility, but New Risk Controls**  
For fintech platforms that enable fund‑to‑affiliate trades, the expanded list of eligible securities could open up new revenue streams and improve liquidity management. However, the SEC’s emphasis on “fairness and transparency” means firms must tighten transaction monitoring, document pricing methodology, and ensure that any affiliate trade is disclosed to investors in a timely manner. Failure to do so could trigger the same enforcement scrutiny that landed the former Western Asset CIO.  

**Enforcement Reminder – Personal Gain Is Not a Grey Area**  
The consent judgment against Ken Leech underscores that the SEC will continue to pursue “cherry‑picking” behavior, even when it occurs behind the scenes of a large, well‑regulated firm. The case sends a clear message: any trade that benefits an employee or affiliate at the expense of the fund’s investors will be investigated, and the agency is prepared to seek final judgments that can include disgorgement and bar orders. Compliance teams must revisit personal‑trading policies, ensure robust conflict‑of‑interest reviews, and reinforce training on prohibited self‑dealing.  

**Outreach Seminar – A One‑Stop Resource**  
The November 19 virtual seminar is a rare chance to hear SEC staff explain how they expect firms to apply the new cross‑trading and crypto‑custody proposals. Attendance will give compliance officers direct insight into the agency’s priorities, helping them anticipate regulator‑driven changes before formal rules are finalized.  

**Fraud‑Awareness Push – Investor Education Is a Compliance Duty**  
The joint fraud‑awareness bulletin expands the SEC’s focus from enforcement to prevention. Fintech firms that provide investor‑facing portals, robo‑advice, or educational content now have a higher expectation to incorporate the bulletin’s warnings into client communications. Ignoring the bulletin could be viewed as a lapse in the firm’s duty to protect investors from known scams.  

**Crypto Custody – Clarity, but New Obligations**  
The crypto‑custody proposal finally gives registered advisers and funds a clear regulatory pathway to hold digital assets, but it also adds concrete obligations: (1) detailed custodial agreements, (2) segregation of client assets, (3) periodic reporting of holdings, and (4) enhanced cybersecurity controls. Fintech providers that offer custodial technology or third‑party custody services must be ready to map their systems to these requirements or risk being deemed non‑compliant.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review and Update Cross‑Trading Policies**  
  - Map the SEC’s proposed eligible securities against your current trade‑execution platform.  
  - Add a documented pricing‑fairness test for any affiliate transaction.  
  - Ensure real‑time disclosure to investors and maintain an audit trail for regulator review.  

- **Audit Conflict‑of‑Interest Controls**  
  - Conduct a rapid, firm‑wide review of personal‑trading and “cherry‑picking” safeguards.  
  - Verify that all employee‑initiated trades are pre‑cleared and that any benefit to insiders is fully disclosed.  
  - Refresh training modules with a case study of the Western Asset judgment to illustrate prohibited conduct.  

- **Plan Attendance for the Nov 19 Seminar**  
  - Register the compliance team and key technology stakeholders now; the session is limited‑capacity.  
  - Prepare a short list of questions on cross‑trading and crypto custody to maximize the value of the Q&A.  

- **Integrate Fraud‑Awareness Messaging**  
  - Incorporate the World Investor Week bulletin language into client‑facing emails, portal alerts, and onboarding materials.  
  - Set up a monitoring rule for known fraud patterns (e.g., phishing, pump‑and‑dump) and trigger alerts for client‑service reps.  

- **Begin Crypto‑Custody Gap Analysis**  
  - Identify all crypto holdings under advisory or fund accounts and map them to the proposed custodial requirements.  
  - Evaluate current custodial agreements for segregation, insurance, and reporting clauses; flag any gaps.  
  - Coordinate with your technology team to ensure that custody platforms can produce the detailed reports the SEC will soon require.  

By acting on these five steps now, compliance teams can stay ahead of the SEC’s evolving rules, reduce enforcement risk, and position their fintech solutions as trustworthy partners for regulated investment firms.
