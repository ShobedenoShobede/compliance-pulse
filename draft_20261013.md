**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils Sweeping Proposals – Expanded Cross‑Trading, Crypto Custody Rules, and New Compliance Guidance – What Fintech Teams Must Address Now  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items today that together reshape the compliance landscape for investment funds, advisers, and fintech platforms that handle securities or crypto assets.  

1. **Cross‑Trading Expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the list of securities that a registered fund may trade with its affiliates, provided the transaction meets existing fairness and disclosure standards.  

2. **Enforcement Action – “Cherry‑Picking”** – The agency moved for a final judgment, by consent, against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The judgment resolves a “cherry‑picking” case in which Leech allegedly steered profitable trades to a personal account while the fund’s investors bore the loss.  

3. **Compliance Outreach Seminar** – The SEC announced a virtual national seminar for investment companies and advisers on November 19, 2026. The session is designed to help chief compliance officers and senior staff understand recent rule changes and best‑practice expectations.  

4. **World Investor Week Fraud Bulletin** – In coordination with global regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin during World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights rising fraud schemes, especially those targeting digital‑asset investors, and urges heightened vigilance.  

5. **Crypto‑Custody Framework** – The commission proposed a new set of rules that would give registered investment advisers and regulated funds a clear, tailored pathway to custody crypto assets. The proposal outlines licensing, risk‑management, and reporting requirements that differ from the current, fragmented approach.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑Trading Changes Affect Every Fund‑Affiliate Relationship**  
Fintech platforms that facilitate internal trading, provide liquidity services, or act as custodians for affiliated entities must revisit their transaction policies. By expanding the eligible securities, the SEC is effectively widening the scope of activities that could trigger cross‑trading scrutiny. Even if your firm’s current trades fall within the old list, the new proposal could bring additional asset classes—such as certain structured products or emerging‑market equities—under the rule. Non‑compliant trades could be deemed “unfair” and lead to enforcement action, especially if they lack transparent pricing or adequate disclosure to investors.  

**Enforcement Signals Zero Tolerance for “Cherry‑Picking”**  
The Western Asset case underscores that the SEC will pursue personal‑gain misconduct aggressively, even when the wrongdoing occurred years ago. The judgment serves as a reminder that any employee who directs profitable opportunities to a private account while the fund suffers a loss is exposing the firm to civil penalties and reputational damage. Fintech firms that enable trade‑execution or portfolio‑management services must ensure robust monitoring of employee trades and enforce strict segregation of personal and client activity.  

**Seminar Offers a One‑Stop Compliance Refresh**  
The upcoming virtual seminar is the SEC’s way of delivering guidance on the very proposals announced today. Attendance will give compliance officers direct access to regulators’ interpretations, practical examples, and a chance to ask questions. Skipping the session could leave teams guessing about how the SEC expects the new rules to be applied, increasing the risk of inadvertent violations.  

**Fraud Bulletin Highlights Growing Threats to Digital‑Asset Users**  
The joint fraud bulletin warns that scams targeting crypto investors are evolving faster than regulatory responses. For fintech firms that offer crypto wallets, trading interfaces, or advisory services, the bulletin is a clear signal that regulators expect heightened consumer‑protection measures—such as clearer risk disclosures, stronger authentication, and rapid response to suspicious activity.  

**Crypto‑Custody Proposal Sets the First Comprehensive Rules**  
Until now, advisers and funds have relied on a patchwork of state money‑transmitter licenses, self‑regulatory organization guidance, and internal policies to hold crypto. The SEC’s proposal creates a unified federal framework, defining who may act as a custodian, what risk‑management controls are required, and how holdings must be reported. Firms that already custody crypto will need to assess whether they meet the new standards; those considering entry into crypto custody must plan for licensing, capital, and reporting obligations that were previously optional.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review Cross‑Trading Policies** – Map every security your firm trades with affiliates. Identify any that will become newly eligible under the proposal and update internal approvals, pricing benchmarks, and disclosure templates accordingly.  

- **Audit Employee Trade Activity** – Implement or strengthen a system that flags personal trades that mirror client transactions. Require pre‑clearance for any employee‑initiated trades in securities that the firm trades on behalf of clients.  

- **Register for the November 19 Seminar** – Add the event to your compliance calendar and assign a senior compliance officer to attend. Capture any Q&A that clarifies the SEC’s expectations for cross‑trading, crypto custody, and fraud prevention.  

- **Enhance Fraud Controls for Digital Assets** – Upgrade client onboarding to include multi‑factor authentication, real‑time transaction monitoring, and clear risk warnings for crypto products. Draft a rapid‑response playbook for suspected fraud incidents, referencing the World Investor Week bulletin.  

- **Begin Crypto‑Custody Gap Analysis** – Compare your current custody practices against the key elements of the SEC’s proposed framework: licensing, capital reserves, segregation of assets, and reporting. Prioritize any gaps that could prevent you from offering custodial services under the new rules and develop a remediation timeline.  

- **Update Investor Disclosures** – Revise prospectuses, offering memoranda, and client agreements to reflect the expanded cross‑trading scope and the firm’s crypto‑custody approach. Ensure language is clear, concise, and highlights any material risks.  

- **Engage Legal Counsel Early** – Because the proposals are still in comment period, work with your legal team to submit feedback on any provisions that may be impractical or overly burdensome for your business model. Early engagement can shape final rules and demonstrate proactive compliance.  

By taking these steps now, fintech compliance teams can stay ahead of the SEC’s evolving expectations, protect their firms from enforcement risk, and position themselves to capture new opportunities in the expanding digital‑asset market.
