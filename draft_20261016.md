**HEADLINE**  
SEC Rolls Out Five Key Initiatives – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track Before Year‑End  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements this week that signal a shift in how the agency will oversee capital formation, fund trading, adviser conduct, compliance education, and investor fraud awareness.  

1. **IPO Modernization & Crowdfunding** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on October 27 to continue its review of the rules governing initial public offerings and Regulation Crowdfunding. The committee is expected to discuss possible updates that could make it easier for small companies to go public while tightening investor protection.  

2. **Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – The Commission proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act that would broaden the types of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliated parties. The proposal seeks to clarify when such “cross‑trades” are permissible, aiming to reduce the risk of conflicts of interest.  

3. **Enforcement Action – Cherry‑Picking** – The SEC filed a motion for final judgment, by consent, against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The case involves “cherry‑picking,” where a portfolio manager allegedly directed favored securities to a private fund he controlled, violating fiduciary duties.  

4. **National Compliance Outreach Seminar** – A virtual seminar for investment companies and advisers is scheduled for November 19. The event will focus on practical steps chief compliance officers can take to meet the agency’s expectations, especially around risk monitoring and reporting.  

5. **World Investor Week Fraud Bulletin** – In partnership with global regulators, the SEC issued a joint investor bulletin to raise fraud awareness during World Investor Week. The bulletin highlights emerging scams, especially those targeting digital‑asset investors, and offers guidance on how firms can help protect their clients.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Capital‑formation rules could affect fintech platforms that facilitate crowdfunding and direct listings.** If the advisory committee adopts more permissive IPO thresholds or tighter disclosure standards for Regulation Crowdfunding, platforms that host equity‑based campaigns will need to adjust onboarding, investor verification, and reporting processes. Early alignment with any new guidance can prevent costly retrofits and keep your platform attractive to issuers.  

**Expanded cross‑trading permissions create new compliance checkpoints for fund‑related fintech services.** Many fintech applications now offer “fund‑of‑funds” or secondary‑market features that rely on intra‑fund trades. The proposed rule change means firms must revisit internal controls that govern which securities can be exchanged between a fund and its affiliates. Failure to implement robust monitoring could expose the firm to enforcement risk and damage client trust.  

**The Western Asset judgment underscores heightened scrutiny of personal trading and fund‑allocation practices.** Even though the case involved a traditional asset manager, the underlying conduct—using private information to benefit a side vehicle—mirrors risks in fintech environments where algorithms or APIs may inadvertently favor related parties. Compliance teams should treat the settlement as a warning sign that the SEC will pursue “cherry‑picking” behavior aggressively, regardless of the business model.  

**The upcoming compliance seminar is a rare chance to hear the SEC’s priorities straight from regulators.** Participation can provide clarity on how the agency expects fintech firms to handle emerging risks such as real‑time data monitoring, AI‑driven advice, and cross‑border data flows. The insights shared will likely influence future examination focus areas, making the seminar a strategic investment of time.  

**The fraud bulletin highlights the growing threat of digital‑asset scams, a space where many fintech firms operate.** Regulators are signaling that they will not tolerate lax anti‑fraud controls, especially when firms market tokenized securities or crypto‑based investment products. Proactive fraud‑prevention measures will become a compliance baseline rather than a differentiator.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review and update your crowdfunding onboarding flow.** Map each data point the SEC is likely to require—issuer disclosures, investor accreditation checks, and ongoing reporting—and ensure your platform can capture and retain that information automatically.  

- **Audit cross‑trading functionalities in fund‑related services.** Verify that any intra‑fund securities transfers are limited to the categories the SEC currently permits. If your system allows broader trades, add rule‑based blocks and document the rationale for any exceptions.  

- **Strengthen personal‑trading policies for staff and algorithmic trading teams.** Require pre‑clearance for any trades that involve securities linked to the firm’s own products or affiliated funds. Implement real‑time monitoring to flag “cherry‑picking” patterns and conduct quarterly reviews.  

- **Register key staff for the November 19 compliance seminar.** Assign at least one senior compliance officer to attend and prepare a list of questions specific to fintech operations—especially around AI‑driven advice and cross‑border data handling. Summarize the takeaways for the broader team within two weeks of the event.  

- **Enhance fraud‑prevention controls for digital‑asset offerings.** Deploy multi‑factor authentication for all investor accounts, integrate real‑time transaction screening against known scam signatures, and publish a clear investor‑education page that mirrors the SEC’s World Investor Week bulletin language.  

By addressing these five areas now, your compliance program will be better positioned to meet the SEC’s evolving expectations, reduce enforcement risk, and maintain client confidence as the regulatory landscape continues to shift.
