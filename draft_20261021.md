**Headline**  
SEC‑AMF MOU, New IPO‑Crowdfunding Talk, Cross‑Trading Rule Change, Enforcement Action, and Compliance Seminar: What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track This Week  

---

### What Happened  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) announced five separate items on October 21, 2026 that affect anyone who offers investment advice, runs a fund, or facilitates capital formation through technology.  

1. **SEC‑AMF Memorandum of Understanding** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed an MOU to streamline information‑sharing and joint supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France. The agreement covers routine supervisory matters, cross‑border investigations, and coordinated responses to market misconduct.  

2. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on October 27 to discuss two topics: modernizing the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) process and revisiting Regulation Crowdfunding. The agenda signals the agency’s intent to make it easier for small companies to raise capital, possibly by tweaking disclosure requirements or expanding the investor pool.  

3. **Proposed Expansion of Cross‑Trading Eligibility** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. Currently, a registered fund may trade securities with its affiliates only under narrow conditions. The proposal would broaden the types of securities that qualify, allowing more flexible intra‑fund transactions while preserving safeguards against conflicts of interest.  

4. **Final Judgment in the “Cherry‑Picking” Case** – The SEC moved for a final judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The case centers on “cherry‑picking” – the practice of allocating the best trades to favored clients while giving poorer execution to others. The judgment underscores the agency’s continued focus on fair‑dealing standards for registered investment advisers.  

5. **National Compliance Outreach Seminar** – The SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program will host a virtual seminar on November 19 for investment companies and advisers. The event is designed to help chief compliance officers (CCOs) and their teams navigate recent regulatory developments, including the cross‑trading proposal and the upcoming IPO‑crowdfunding discussions.  

---

### Why It Matters  

**Cross‑border supervision gets tighter** – The SEC‑AMF MOU means that U.S.‑based fintech firms that serve French investors—or French platforms that offer U.S. securities—can expect more coordinated examinations. Information that once moved slowly between regulators will now be exchanged more quickly. For compliance teams, this raises the stakes for maintaining consistent policies across jurisdictions, especially around data protection, anti‑money‑laundering (AML) checks, and client onboarding.  

**Small‑business capital formation may shift** – The advisory committee’s focus on IPO modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding suggests that the SEC could relax some disclosure thresholds or expand the types of securities that can be offered through online portals. Fintech platforms that already host equity crowdfunding or that plan to add IPO services should watch for rule changes that could affect investor qualification, reporting timelines, and marketing communications. Early preparation can turn a regulatory change into a competitive advantage.  

**Cross‑trading flexibility, but with new risk controls** – Allowing a broader set of securities to be traded between a fund and its affiliates could simplify portfolio rebalancing and improve liquidity for multi‑manager structures. However, the SEC’s emphasis on “conflict‑of‑interest safeguards” means that firms must document why each intra‑fund trade is fair, how pricing is determined, and how the trade is reported. Failure to do so could trigger enforcement actions similar to the cherry‑picking case.  

**Enforcement stays aggressive on unfair trade allocation** – The final judgment against the former Western Asset CIO sends a clear reminder that the SEC will pursue misconduct that harms investors, even when the wrongdoing occurs behind the scenes. Fintech advisers that use algorithmic order routing, best‑execution analytics, or third‑party execution services must ensure those tools do not inadvertently favor certain accounts. Documentation, regular testing, and transparent client disclosures are now non‑negotiable.  

**Compliance education is being centralized** – The November 19 virtual seminar is an opportunity to get direct guidance from SEC staff on the very issues listed above. Attendance is free and the session will be recorded. For teams that struggle to keep up with the pace of rulemaking, the seminar can serve as a concise briefing and a chance to ask specific questions about implementation.  

---

### What to Do  

- **Map cross‑border activities** – Create a quick inventory of any French‑related clients, data flows, or partner platforms. Verify that your AML, Know‑Your‑Customer (KYC), and privacy policies meet both SEC and AMF expectations.  

- **Review crowdfunding and IPO roadmaps** – If your platform enables Regulation Crowdfunding or plans to add IPO services, draft a checklist of upcoming disclosure and investor‑qualification requirements. Flag any gaps now so you can adapt when the SEC finalizes its proposals.  

- **Update cross‑trading procedures** – Draft or revise internal guidelines to cover the expanded list of eligible securities. Include a step‑by‑step approval workflow, pricing methodology, and a record‑keeping schedule that satisfies the SEC’s conflict‑of‑interest concerns.  

- **Audit best‑execution controls** – Run a focused review of order‑routing logic, execution quality metrics, and client‑level reporting. Document how the system treats all accounts equally and be ready to produce that evidence in an examination.  

- **Register for the November 19 seminar** – Assign a senior compliance officer to attend (or watch the recording) and prepare a short list of questions about the cross‑trading rule change, the IPO‑crowdfunding agenda, and any AMF‑related supervisory expectations. Disseminate key takeaways to the broader team within 48 hours of the event.  

These steps will help your compliance function stay ahead of the SEC’s latest moves, reduce the risk of enforcement, and position your fintech platform to benefit from any regulatory easing in the small‑business capital‑raising space.
