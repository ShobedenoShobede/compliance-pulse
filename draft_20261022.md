**HEADLINE**  
SEC & French AMF Sign MOU, Push IPO Modernization, Expand Cross‑Trading, and Ramp Up Compliance Outreach – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track This Week  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and France’s market regulator, the Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF), announced a new Memorandum of Understanding (MOU). The agreement formalizes a framework for regular consultation, information‑sharing, and coordinated supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France. The MOU is intended to speed up the exchange of supervisory findings, reduce duplicated examinations, and help both agencies address cross‑border risks more efficiently.  

In parallel, the SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on 27 October 2026 to continue its work on modernizing the initial public offering (IPO) process and to explore reforms to Regulation Crowdfunding. The agenda includes discussion of streamlined filing requirements, expanded use of electronic “deal‑rooms,” and potential adjustments to the $5 million annual fundraising cap for non‑accredited investors.  

The SEC also released a proposed rule change to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading provisions. Currently, registered funds may only trade a narrow set of securities with affiliates under strict conditions. The proposal would broaden the list of eligible securities, relax certain timing constraints, and introduce new record‑keeping standards aimed at improving market liquidity while preserving investor protection.  

Separately, the SEC moved for a final judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The judgment resolves a “cherry‑picking” case in which Leech allegedly directed fund trades to benefit personal interests at the expense of investors. The consent order includes monetary penalties, a ban on serving as an officer or director of a registered investment adviser, and a requirement to cooperate with future investigations.  

Finally, the SEC’s Compliance Outreach Program announced a virtual national seminar for investment companies and investment advisers on 19 November 2026. The two‑hour session will focus on best practices for risk governance, cyber‑security, and emerging regulatory trends. The event is open to chief compliance officers, chief risk officers, and senior compliance staff.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑border supervision gets a boost.** The SEC‑AMF MOU means regulators will share findings more quickly when a fintech firm offers advisory or custodial services in both the U.S. and France. For compliance teams, this translates into a higher likelihood that a supervisory issue identified in Paris will surface in a U.S. examination, and vice‑versa. Companies that have not yet mapped their global supervisory footprint may face surprise requests for documentation or corrective actions from either regulator.  

**IPO and crowdfunding reforms could reshape fundraising.** If the advisory committee’s recommendations lead to a lighter IPO filing burden and a higher crowdfunding ceiling, fintech platforms that facilitate equity offerings will need to adjust their onboarding, disclosure, and investor‑verification workflows. A broader fundraising cap also means more retail participation, raising the stakes for anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and know‑your‑customer (KYC) controls on a larger, more diverse investor base.  

**Expanded cross‑trading rules affect fund‑of‑funds and liquid‑alternative strategies.** The proposed rule would let registered funds trade a wider array of equities, corporate bonds, and certain derivatives with affiliated entities. While the change promises greater liquidity and potentially lower transaction costs, it also creates new compliance checkpoints: firms must document the “fair‑value” justification for each trade, maintain real‑time audit trails, and ensure that affiliate trades do not disadvantage shareholders. Failure to meet the new record‑keeping standards could trigger enforcement actions.  

**Enforcement precedent on cherry‑picking sends a warning.** The Leech judgment underscores that the SEC will pursue senior personnel who facilitate self‑dealing, even when the conduct occurs within a large, well‑regulated asset‑manager. The penalties—especially the bar on future leadership roles—signal that personal misconduct will be scrutinized closely. Fintech firms that employ portfolio managers, algorithm designers, or advisory staff must reinforce internal controls around trade approvals and conflict‑of‑interest disclosures.  

**Outreach seminar offers a quick compliance refresher.** The November 19 virtual seminar is a low‑cost way for senior compliance staff to get up to speed on the SEC’s current enforcement focus, especially around cyber‑risk, data‑privacy, and the new cross‑trading proposals. Attendance can help teams anticipate regulator questions before they arise in examinations or investigations.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map your supervisory matrix.** Create or update a spreadsheet that lists every regulator with jurisdiction over your firm’s activities (U.S., EU, UK, etc.). Note the points of contact, recent examination dates, and any pending MOU‑related information‑sharing agreements. Share the matrix with senior management and legal counsel.  

- **Review IPO and crowdfunding workflows.** Conduct a gap analysis of your platform’s filing templates, investor‑verification procedures, and electronic data‑rooms against the advisory committee’s discussion topics. Identify any steps that could be streamlined once the SEC adopts the proposed reforms.  

- **Update cross‑trading policies.** If your firm trades securities with affiliates, draft a checklist that captures the new eligibility criteria, fair‑value analysis, and audit‑trail requirements. Train portfolio managers and compliance analysts on the checklist and embed it into your order‑management system.  

- **Strengthen conflict‑of‑interest controls.** Institute a mandatory, quarterly attestation for senior investment staff confirming that no personal trades have been executed that could be viewed as cherry‑picking. Pair the attestation with automated monitoring of trade logs to flag any deviation from approved affiliate‑trade parameters.  

- **Register for the SEC compliance seminar.** Assign a senior compliance officer to attend the November 19 virtual event and prepare a one‑page briefing for the broader team. Capture any actionable insights—especially around cyber‑security best practices—and incorporate them into your next risk‑assessment cycle.  

- **Enhance documentation for regulator requests.** Anticipate that the SEC and AMF may request the same information in parallel. Consolidate your policies, procedures, and trade records into a centralized repository that can be accessed securely by both U.S. and French teams. Use version control to track changes and maintain a clear audit trail.  

- **Monitor the rulemaking docket.** Subscribe to SEC alerts for the cross‑trading proposal (File No. 23‑112) and set calendar reminders for comment deadlines. Draft a position paper that outlines any operational concerns your firm has with the proposed changes; early comments can influence final rule language and demonstrate proactive engagement.
