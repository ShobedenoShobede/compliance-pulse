**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils Cross‑Border Cooperation, Crowdfunding Push, and Fund‑Trading Rule Change – Immediate Compliance Tasks for Fintech Firms

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) released a flurry of announcements on October 23, 2026 that touch every corner of the fintech compliance landscape. First, the SEC signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The agreement formalizes a framework for information‑sharing and joint supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the U.S. and French markets.  

Next, the SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27 to debate “IPO modernization” and potential regulatory tweaks to Regulation Crowdfunding. The committee will explore ways to streamline public offerings for smaller companies, including adjustments to disclosure thresholds and the use of digital platforms.  

In a separate rule‑making initiative, the SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The change would broaden the types of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliated entities, aiming to improve liquidity and reduce transaction costs.  

The agency also moved for a final judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑CIO of Western Asset Management, in a “cherry‑picking” case that alleged he steered client assets into securities that benefited him personally. The consent judgment underscores the SEC’s continued focus on fiduciary breaches.  

Finally, the SEC announced a virtual National Compliance Outreach Seminar for investment companies and advisers on November 19. The session is designed to help chief compliance officers navigate the agency’s latest expectations, with a particular emphasis on risk‑based compliance programs and emerging technology.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Cross‑border oversight will tighten.** The SEC‑AMF MOU means that any fintech platform offering advisory or custodial services to U.S. and French clients must be ready for coordinated examinations. Data requests, supervisory findings, and enforcement actions could now be shared between the two regulators, raising the stakes for gaps in AML/KYC, client reporting, and record‑keeping. Firms that have previously treated U.S. and EU compliance as separate silos will need to harmonize policies, especially around data privacy (GDPR vs. U.S. privacy rules) and the handling of “investment‑service provider” activities.  

**Crowdfunding reforms could open new distribution channels.** The advisory committee’s focus on modernizing IPOs and Regulation Crowdfunding signals that the SEC may lower the $5 million fundraising cap or relax investor‑qualification rules. Fintech platforms that facilitate equity crowdfunding should monitor the meeting outcomes closely. Early adoption of any relaxed standards could give a competitive edge, but premature changes without SEC guidance could expose firms to inadvertent violations.  

**Expanded cross‑trading eligibility changes fund‑operations risk.** By allowing a wider class of securities to be traded between a fund and its affiliates, the SEC is effectively loosening a long‑standing safeguard against conflicts of interest. Fintech fund managers and custodians must reassess internal controls, pricing models, and disclosure practices to ensure that any cross‑trades remain at fair market value and are fully documented. Failure to do so could trigger enforcement actions similar to the cherry‑picking case.  

**Enforcement remains aggressive on fiduciary breaches.** The judgment against the former Western Asset CIO demonstrates that the SEC will continue to pursue “cherry‑picking” and other self‑dealing schemes, even when the misconduct is years old. Compliance teams should audit historical trade allocations, personal‑account monitoring, and conflict‑of‑interest disclosures to confirm that no similar patterns exist in their own portfolios.  

**Outreach resources are now available.** The upcoming virtual seminar provides a direct line to SEC staff on how they expect firms to implement the new cross‑trading rules, handle crowdfunding offerings, and manage cross‑border supervision. Attending or reviewing the seminar materials will help compliance officers align their programs with the regulator’s current thinking, reducing the likelihood of surprise examinations.

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map cross‑border data flows.** Conduct a rapid inventory of all client data, transaction records, and reporting processes that involve U.S. and French jurisdictions. Identify any gaps between SEC and AMF expectations (e.g., GDPR‑style consent, data‑retention periods) and draft a remediation plan within 30 days.  

- **Update crowdfunding product roadmaps.** Assign a product owner to track the Small Business Advisory Committee’s meeting minutes and any forthcoming rule changes. Prepare a “what‑if” analysis for lower fundraising caps or expanded investor eligibility, and adjust platform onboarding flows accordingly.  

- **Review cross‑trading policies.** Assemble a cross‑functional task force (legal, fund operations, risk) to evaluate the proposed amendment’s impact on your fund‑management tools. Verify that pricing algorithms, fair‑value calculations, and audit trails can accommodate the broader securities list; document any required system upgrades.  

- **Audit historical trade allocations.** Run a compliance query on all trade allocation records from the past five years for signs of preferential treatment or mismatched pricing. If anomalies are found, conduct a root‑cause analysis, remediate the issue, and enhance monitoring controls to prevent future cherry‑picking.  

- **Leverage the November 19 seminar.** Register the chief compliance officer and at least one senior compliance analyst for the virtual outreach event. After the session, circulate a concise briefing to the broader compliance team, highlighting any actionable guidance from SEC staff and updating internal checklists accordingly.
