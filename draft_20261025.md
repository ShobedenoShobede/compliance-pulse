**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils 2027 Exam Focus, Expands Cross‑Trading Rules, and Tightens Oversight of Advisers – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Know Today  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released its Division of Examinations’ priorities for Fiscal Year 2027. The agency highlighted three core themes: (1) the integrity of digital‑asset and fintech platforms, (2) the effectiveness of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and cyber‑security controls, and (3) the quality of governance and risk‑management at registered investment advisers (RIAs) and broker‑dealers. The report also flags heightened scrutiny of “cross‑trading” practices by registered funds and the use of non‑public information in trading decisions.  

In parallel, the SEC signed a supervisory memorandum of understanding (MOU) with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The MOU formalises a framework for information‑sharing and joint supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across U.S. and French markets. It is the latest step in a broader effort to coordinate cross‑border oversight of fintech firms that serve multinational clients.  

Other notable actions include:  

* The Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on Oct. 27 to discuss modernising the IPO process and possible reforms to Regulation Crowdfunding, signaling a potential shift toward faster, lower‑cost public offerings for fintech startups.  
* The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule, expanding the types of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliates. The proposal aims to increase liquidity but also raises concerns about conflicts of interest and market fairness.  
* The Commission moved for a final judgment against former Western Asset co‑CIO Stephen Kenneth Leech II for “cherry‑picking” – selectively allocating fund assets to benefit certain clients. The case underscores the SEC’s continued focus on fiduciary breaches and improper trade allocations.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Examination priorities set the compliance agenda.**  
Fintech firms that are registered as broker‑dealers, RIAs, or operate crypto‑exchange platforms will likely find themselves in the SEC’s examination queue this year. The emphasis on digital‑asset integrity means that any gaps in customer‑on‑boarding, transaction monitoring, or data‑security could trigger a supervisory review. Even firms that do not currently hold a securities licence should watch the guidance, because the SEC often expands its reach to ancillary service providers that touch the securities market.  

**2. Cross‑border cooperation raises the bar for multinational fintechs.**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU creates a formal channel for sharing inspection findings, enforcement actions, and best‑practice guidance. Companies that serve both U.S. and European clients must now anticipate coordinated inquiries. A weakness identified by the AMF could be relayed to the SEC, and vice‑versa, potentially resulting in simultaneous enforcement in both jurisdictions.  

**3. Expanded cross‑trading permissions alter fund‑management risk.**  
The proposed rule change would let registered funds trade a broader set of securities with affiliates, including certain derivatives and structured products. While the SEC argues this will improve liquidity, it also opens the door to more complex conflict‑of‑interest scenarios. Fintech platforms that facilitate fund‑to‑fund or fund‑to‑client transactions need to revisit their internal controls, especially around price‑disclosure, best‑execution testing, and independent oversight of affiliate trades.  

**4. IPO and crowdfunding reforms could accelerate fintech fundraising.**  
If the Advisory Committee’s recommendations lead to a streamlined IPO pathway or a higher fundraising cap under Regulation Crowdfunding, fintech startups may see faster access to public capital. However, a more rapid route also means tighter disclosure expectations and a shorter window for due‑diligence. Compliance teams must be ready to produce high‑quality, investor‑ready documentation on a tighter timeline.  

**5. Enforcement actions reinforce fiduciary standards.**  
The cherry‑picking judgment against a former co‑CIO sends a clear message: the SEC will pursue individuals and firms that manipulate trade allocations for personal or client benefit. Fintech advisers and robo‑advisors must ensure that trade‑allocation algorithms are transparent, documented, and regularly audited. Any deviation could be interpreted as a breach of fiduciary duty.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map your examination exposure.** Conduct a rapid gap analysis against the FY 2027 focus areas—digital‑asset controls, AML/cyber‑security, and governance. Prioritise remediation of any missing policies, especially those related to transaction monitoring and data‑security for crypto‑related activities.  

- **Review cross‑border data‑sharing protocols.** If you operate in the U.S. and EU, audit your information‑exchange processes to ensure they meet both SEC and AMF expectations. Update your privacy notices and data‑processing agreements to reflect the possibility of joint supervisory reviews.  

- **Re‑evaluate cross‑trading safeguards.** For any fund‑related services, tighten conflict‑of‑interest checks. Implement independent pricing verification for affiliate trades and document the rationale for each transaction. Consider adding a “dual‑approval” workflow where a compliance officer, not just a portfolio manager, signs off on cross‑trading activity.  

- **Prepare for a faster IPO/crowdfunding pipeline.** Draft a checklist of required disclosures, financial statements, and governance documents that can be quickly assembled for a modernised IPO or a Regulation Crowdfunding filing. Run mock reviews with your legal team to identify bottlenecks before a formal filing is needed.  

- **Audit trade‑allocation logic.** If you use algorithms to allocate orders across client accounts, perform an independent audit to confirm that the process treats all investors fairly and consistently. Document the methodology, test results, and any exceptions. Keep these records readily accessible for potential SEC inquiries.  

By acting now, your compliance program can stay ahead of the SEC’s 2027 agenda, reduce the risk of cross‑border enforcement, and position your fintech firm to benefit from upcoming market‑structure changes.
