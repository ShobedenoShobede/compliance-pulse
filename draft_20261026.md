**SECURE THE FUTURE: Five New SEC Moves That Will Shape Fintech Compliance in 2027**

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released a flurry of announcements this week that together signal a tighter, more collaborative regulatory environment for firms that operate in the fintech space.  

1. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The SEC’s Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year‑2027 focus areas. The agency highlighted three core themes: (a) the integrity of investment‑adviser and broker‑dealer operations, (b) the use of technology and data analytics in compliance, and (c) the protection of retail investors in digital‑asset and crowdfunding offerings. The report also listed specific red‑flag behaviors—such as inadequate supervisory controls, insufficient record‑keeping, and weak cybersecurity—that examiners will target in upcoming inspections.  

2. **U.S.–France Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers. The MOU covers joint monitoring of cross‑border activities, coordinated investigations, and the exchange of best‑practice guidance on emerging risks, especially those tied to digital platforms and crypto‑related services.  

3. **Small‑Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on Oct. 27 to discuss two hot topics: modernizing the initial public offering (IPO) process and refining Regulation Crowdfunding rules. The agenda includes proposals to streamline registration, reduce filing burdens, and expand the types of securities that can be offered through crowdfunding portals.  

4. **Proposed Expansion of Cross‑Trading Rules** – The agency proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the categories of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliates, provided the transactions meet “fair‑value” and “best‑interest” standards. The SEC is seeking public comment on whether the expanded scope could create conflicts of interest or undermine investor protection.  

5. **Final Judgment in “Cherry‑Picking” Case** – The SEC moved for a final judgment against former Western Asset co‑CIO Ken Leech for “cherry‑picking” – the practice of steering favored securities to certain fund shareholders while disadvantaging others. The settlement underscores the agency’s continued focus on fiduciary breaches and reinforces the expectation that advisers maintain strict segregation of client interests.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

Fintech firms—whether they run robo‑advisors, crypto exchanges, crowdfunding portals, or data‑analytics platforms for brokers—must treat these five developments as a single, coordinated shift in the regulator’s playbook.  

1. **Higher Examination Risk** – The FY 2027 priorities make clear that the SEC will scrutinize technology‑driven compliance programs more aggressively. Firms that rely on automated monitoring, AI‑based risk scoring, or cloud‑hosted data warehouses must be able to demonstrate robust controls, audit trails, and incident‑response plans. Failure to do so could trigger a costly examination, reputational damage, or enforcement action.  

2. **Cross‑Border Oversight** – The U.S.–France MOU means that French regulators can now request information from U.S. fintech firms and vice‑versa. If your platform serves European investors, you will be subject to simultaneous review by two agencies. In practice, this translates into tighter reporting requirements, harmonized data‑privacy expectations, and a need for a single point of contact who can field inquiries from both sides of the Atlantic.  

3. **Retail‑Investor Focus** – The upcoming advisory‑committee discussion on IPO modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding signals that the SEC intends to lower barriers for small companies while still protecting everyday investors. Fintech platforms that facilitate equity crowdfunding must be ready for potential rule changes that could affect disclosure formats, investor‑qualification thresholds, and the permissible use of digital‑identity verification. Early alignment with the likely direction of these reforms will give you a competitive edge.  

4. **Expanded Cross‑Trading Flexibility—and Risk** – The proposed rule change could allow registered funds to trade a broader set of securities with affiliates, which may be attractive for fintechs that manage multi‑entity structures or provide “white‑label” fund services. However, the SEC’s emphasis on fair‑value and best‑interest standards means that any such trades must be documented with transparent pricing models and independent oversight. Ignoring these safeguards could expose firms to allegations of self‑dealing.  

5. **Fiduciary Enforcement Remains Aggressive** – The Ken Leech judgment reinforces that the SEC will not tolerate even subtle breaches of fiduciary duty. Fintech advisers that use algorithms to allocate assets must ensure the logic does not inadvertently favor certain client groups. The case also reminds compliance teams that internal whistleblowers and external tips can quickly surface hidden misconduct.  

Overall, the common thread is **greater accountability for technology‑enabled activities**. Regulators are moving from a “watch‑and‑wait” stance to proactive supervision, especially where digital tools intersect with investor protection.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit your tech‑stack now.** Conduct a rapid, documented review of all automated compliance processes (e.g., transaction monitoring, trade‑allocation algorithms, data‑retention systems). Verify that each step logs who did what, when, and why, and that logs are immutable and readily extractable for an examiner.  

- **Design a cross‑border liaison function.** Appoint a senior compliance officer (or team) to handle information requests from both the SEC and foreign regulators such as the AMF. Draft a standard protocol for sharing client data that satisfies U.S. privacy rules (e.g., GDPR equivalence) and French supervisory expectations.  

- **Prep for crowdfunding rule changes.** Map the current end‑to‑end flow of equity‑crowdfunding transactions on your platform. Identify any data fields, disclosure documents, or identity‑verification steps that may need to be expanded or reformatted once the SEC finalizes its modernization proposals.  

- **Update cross‑trading policies.** If your firm conducts any affiliate trades, revise internal policies to incorporate the “fair‑value” and “best‑interest” tests the SEC is emphasizing. Include independent price verification, conflict‑of‑interest disclosures, and a quarterly review by the board’s audit committee.
