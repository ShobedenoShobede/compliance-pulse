**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils FY 2027 Examination Priorities, Expands Cross‑Trading Rules, and Pushes IPO & Crowdfunding Reform – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track Right Now  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released a suite of announcements that together reshape the regulatory landscape for fintech firms, investment advisers, and fund managers.  

1. **Fiscal Year 2027 examination priorities** – The SEC’s Division of Examinations published its annual focus areas. The list highlights “technology risk management,” “cyber‑security,” “environmental, social and governance (ESG) disclosures,” “digital assets,” and “investment‑company governance” as top examination topics. The agency also flagged “financial‑crime prevention” and “operational resilience” as cross‑cutting themes that will be woven into most examinations.  

2. **U.S.–France supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers. The agreement creates a formal channel for the two regulators to consult on cross‑border supervision, joint investigations, and the handling of market‑abuse cases involving U.S. and French firms.  

3. **Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee meeting** – The Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27 2026 to discuss “IPO modernization” and the future of Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying the IPO registration process for emerging companies, expanding the types of securities that can be offered under crowdfunding, and assessing whether current investor‑protection thresholds remain appropriate.  

4. **Proposed expansion of cross‑trading rules** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act that would broaden the categories of securities eligible for cross‑trading between a registered fund and its affiliates. The changes aim to increase liquidity for fund portfolios while preserving safeguards against conflicts of interest.  

5. **Final judgment in a “cherry‑picking” case** – The SEC moved for a final judgment against former Western Asset co‑Chief Investment Officer Stephen Kenneth Leech II. The judgment resolves a long‑running enforcement action alleging that Leech steered profitable trades to a personal account while denying the same opportunities to client portfolios – a classic “cherry‑picking” violation.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Examination priorities set the compliance agenda** – The SEC’s FY 2027 focus list is more than a public statement; it signals the areas examiners will probe most aggressively. Fintech firms that rely on cloud services, AI‑driven analytics, or digital‑asset platforms must be ready to demonstrate robust technology‑risk controls, incident‑response plans, and documentation of any ESG data they collect or disclose. Failure to have these controls documented can trigger a “risk‑based” examination that may delay product launches or lead to enforcement notices.  

**2. Cross‑border supervision is tightening** – The new SEC‑AMF MOU means that French regulators can now request information directly from U.S. firms, and vice‑versa, without the usual diplomatic delays. For fintech companies that serve both U.S. and European clients—whether through a U.S.‑registered adviser, a European subsidiary, or a multi‑jurisdictional platform—this creates a faster, more coordinated enforcement environment. Data‑privacy practices, anti‑money‑laundering (AML) monitoring, and marketing disclosures will be scrutinized under both U.S. and French standards.  

**3. IPO and crowdfunding reforms could reshape fundraising** – The Advisory Committee’s upcoming discussion points to a possible easing of registration requirements for small‑cap IPOs and an expansion of the securities that can be offered through Regulation Crowdfunding. Fintech platforms that facilitate equity crowdfunding should anticipate rule changes that may broaden the investor pool, increase offering sizes, or adjust the “accredited‑investor” thresholds. Early alignment with any new requirements can give platforms a competitive edge and reduce the risk of retroactive compliance gaps.  

**4. Expanded cross‑trading rules affect fund‑of‑funds and liquidity providers** – By allowing a wider range of securities to be cross‑traded, the SEC is effectively permitting more intra‑family transactions that can improve fund liquidity. However, the amendments also tighten the reporting and conflict‑of‑interest safeguards. Fintech solutions that automate fund accounting, trade execution, or portfolio rebalancing must be updated to capture the new eligibility criteria and to generate the required disclosures for each cross‑trade.  

**5. Enforcement precedent on “cherry‑picking” sends a warning** – The final judgment against a former co‑CIO underscores the SEC’s willingness to pursue individual misconduct that harms client portfolios. For fintech firms that provide algorithmic trading tools, robo‑advice, or any discretionary management service, the case reinforces the need for clear segregation of personal and client trades, audit trails, and real‑time monitoring of trade allocation. Even a single instance of preferential treatment can trigger a costly enforcement action.  

Overall, these five items converge on three themes that fintech compliance teams cannot ignore: **technology risk**, **cross‑border regulatory coordination**, and **trade‑allocation integrity**. Ignoring any of these themes can lead to examination findings, enforcement actions, or missed business opportunities as the market evolves.
