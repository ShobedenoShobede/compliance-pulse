**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils FY 2027 Exam Priorities, Expands Cross‑Trading Rules, and Pushes IPO & Crowdfunding Reform – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Address Right Now  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released its Division of Examinations FY 2027 priorities. The agency highlighted three focus areas: (1) oversight of emerging fintech products and services, especially crypto‑related offerings and digital asset custody; (2) enforcement of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and Know‑Your‑Customer (KYC) controls for investment advisers and broker‑dealers using automated onboarding; and (3) scrutiny of fund governance, including cross‑trading practices and conflicts of interest in registered investment companies. The report signals that exam teams will target firms that rely heavily on technology to deliver advisory, custody, or funding services.  

In parallel, the SEC signed a supervisory Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The MOU establishes a formal channel for information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment service providers that operate across the United States and France. It covers joint investigations, coordinated supervisory reviews, and the exchange of best‑practice guidance on cross‑border compliance.  

The Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee met on Oct. 27 to discuss two long‑standing reform topics: modernizing the initial public offering (IPO) process for emerging companies and updating Regulation Crowdfunding rules. Participants examined proposals to streamline prospectus filing, reduce disclosure burdens for “growth‑stage” issuers, and expand the investor‑type eligibility thresholds under crowdfunding.  

Separately, the SEC issued a proposed rule amendment to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading provision. The change would broaden the classes of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliated entities, provided the transactions meet new transparency and pricing safeguards.  

Finally, the Commission moved for a final judgment against Stephen Kenneth Leech II, former co‑Chief Investment Officer of Western Asset Management. The case stems from “cherry‑picking” – the selective allocation of higher‑quality securities to favored client accounts – and concludes the SEC’s enforcement action against a senior investment professional for breach of fiduciary duty.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Heightened exam focus on fintech‑driven activities** – The FY 2027 priorities make clear that the SEC will target firms whose core business is built on digital platforms, APIs, and automated decision‑making. For fintech companies that offer robo‑advisory, tokenized assets, or embedded finance, this translates into deeper scrutiny of algorithmic risk controls, data‑privacy safeguards, and the adequacy of supervisory procedures. A failure to demonstrate robust governance could trigger a formal examination, costly remedial actions, or even civil penalties.  

**2. Cross‑border supervision now formalized** – The SEC‑AMF MOU means that any fintech entity providing investment advice, brokerage, or custodial services to U.S. and French clients must anticipate coordinated oversight. Regulators can now share investigation findings in real time, potentially accelerating enforcement actions. Companies with a presence in both jurisdictions should review their compliance frameworks for consistency, ensuring that policies on client onboarding, data protection, and reporting meet the stricter of the two regimes.  

**3. IPO and crowdfunding reforms could reshape fundraising** – The advisory committee’s discussion signals an imminent regulatory shift that may lower the cost and complexity of going public for fintech startups. If the SEC adopts proposals to simplify prospectus requirements and broaden crowdfunding investor eligibility, companies could raise capital faster and from a larger pool of retail participants. Compliance teams must be ready to adjust disclosure processes, update investor communication templates, and monitor any new qualification thresholds that affect who can invest.  

**4. Expanded cross‑trading eligibility raises governance risk** – While the proposed rule aims to improve liquidity for registered funds, it also widens the scope for intra‑fund transactions that could conceal conflicts of interest. Fintech platforms that facilitate fund‑to‑fund trading or provide automated allocation services must embed additional controls: real‑time pricing verification, independent audit trails, and clear reporting to fund boards. Ignoring these safeguards could expose firms to enforcement under the same “cherry‑picking” doctrine demonstrated in the Western Asset case.  

**5. Enforcement precedent on fiduciary breaches** – The final judgment against the former Western Asset CIO reinforces the SEC’s willingness to pursue senior individuals for preferential treatment of certain accounts. Fintech firms that employ algorithmic trading desks or AI‑driven portfolio construction must ensure that the same investment logic is applied uniformly to all client segments. Any evidence of selective execution could be interpreted as a breach of fiduciary duty, attracting both civil penalties and reputational damage.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map fintech product lines to the FY 2027 exam focus** – Conduct a rapid risk assessment of all digital advisory, custody, and AML/KYC automation tools. Identify gaps in governance, model validation, and data‑security controls, and prioritize remediation before the next scheduled examination.  

- **Align cross‑border compliance policies** – Review your U.S. and French regulatory obligations side by side. Adopt a single set of policies for client onboarding, data retention, and reporting that satisfy the stricter of the two regimes, and document the rationale for any jurisdiction‑specific variations.  

- **Prepare for IPO and crowdfunding rule changes** – Update your capital‑raising playbook to include a streamlined prospectus checklist and a revised investor‑qualification matrix. Train the legal and finance teams on the upcoming thresholds so they can quickly adapt marketing materials and subscription agreements.  

- **Strengthen cross‑trading oversight** – Implement automated pricing checks that compare affiliate transaction prices to market benchmarks in real time. Require independent board approval for any cross‑trade involving newly eligible securities, and retain detailed audit logs for regulator review.  

- **Standardize execution across client segments** – Deploy monitoring tools that flag deviations in trade execution, allocation, or pricing between retail, institutional, and “preferred” accounts. Conduct quarterly reviews of algorithmic decision logs to confirm uniform treatment, and document corrective actions when anomalies are detected.  

By acting now, compliance officers can turn these regulatory developments from a surprise liability into an opportunity to reinforce controls, reduce examination risk, and position their fintech firms for smoother growth in the evolving capital‑markets landscape.
