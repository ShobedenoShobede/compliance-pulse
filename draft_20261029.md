**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils FY 2027 Examination Priorities, Expands Cross‑Trading Rules, and Pushes IPO & Crowdfunding Reform – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Address Right Now  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five major updates this week that together reshape the compliance landscape for fintech firms, investment advisers, and registered funds.  

1. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The SEC’s Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year‑2027 focus areas. The agency will concentrate on: (a) the integrity of digital‑asset platforms, (b) the use of artificial intelligence and machine‑learning in investment decision‑making, (c) anti‑money‑laundering (AML) controls for crypto‑related transactions, (d) the governance of “thin‑capitalized” broker‑dealers, and (e) the adequacy of disclosures for retail investors in “crowd‑funded” offerings.  

2. **U.S.–France Supervisory MOU** – The SEC signed a memorandum of understanding with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The agreement creates a formal channel for sharing information and coordinating oversight of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions.  

3. **Small‑Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee met on Oct. 27 to discuss two agenda items: modernizing the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) process for emerging companies and refining Regulation Crowdfunding rules to improve access while protecting investors.  

4. **Proposed Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – The agency proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the types of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliated entities, provided the trades meet fairness and disclosure standards.  

5. **Final Judgment in Cherry‑Picking Case** – The SEC moved for a final judgment against former Western Asset co‑CIO Ken Leech for “cherry‑picking” – the practice of steering client assets into securities that benefit the adviser’s personal holdings. The judgment underscores the agency’s continued focus on fiduciary breaches.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Examination priorities signal where regulators will look first**  
Fintech platforms that facilitate crypto trading, use AI‑driven advice, or host crowd‑funding campaigns will likely be on the SEC’s exam radar this year. An exam can trigger costly remedial actions, reputational damage, or enforcement referrals. Knowing the priority list lets compliance officers pre‑emptively audit the most vulnerable processes—especially data‑integrity controls for digital assets and AML monitoring for crypto transactions.  

**2. Cross‑border cooperation raises the stakes for multinational fintechs**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU means that information about a firm’s activities in the United States can be shared with French regulators, and vice‑versa. For fintechs that serve U.S. and European investors, a compliance lapse in one market could quickly become a dual‑jurisdiction issue. The MOU also creates a pathway for coordinated enforcement, which can increase the speed and severity of penalties.  

**3. IPO modernization and crowdfunding reforms affect fundraising pathways**  
The advisory committee’s discussion foreshadows possible rule changes that could lower the cost and complexity of going public for tech‑focused firms. At the same time, any tightening of Regulation Crowdfunding rules—such as higher disclosure thresholds or stricter investor‑qualification tests—will impact how fintechs raise capital from retail participants. Early alignment with the committee’s thinking can help firms stay ahead of new filing requirements.  

**4. Expanded cross‑trading rules create both opportunity and risk**  
Allowing a wider range of securities to be traded between a fund and its affiliates can improve liquidity and reduce transaction costs for registered funds. However, the rule still demands that trades be “fair and reasonable” and that any conflicts be disclosed. Fintechs that provide fund‑administration services or develop automated trading tools must embed robust conflict‑of‑interest checks and transparent reporting to avoid violating the expanded rule.  

**5. The cherry‑picking judgment reinforces fiduciary expectations**  
The SEC’s swift move to a final judgment in the Leech case sends a clear message: advisers cannot profit from client assets without full disclosure and client consent. Fintech advisers that rely on algorithmic portfolio construction or that offer “managed account” services must ensure that any internal trades benefiting the firm are fully documented, disclosed, and, where required, approved by the client.  

Overall, these updates converge on three themes that fintech compliance teams cannot ignore: (a) heightened scrutiny of digital‑asset and AI‑driven activities, (b) greater regulatory coordination across borders, and (c) stricter expectations around transparency, conflicts, and investor protection.  

---

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Run a focused risk assessment on digital‑asset and AI functions**  
  * Map every system that trades, holds, or prices crypto or uses machine‑learning models.  
  * Verify that AML/KYC controls capture the full transaction lifecycle, including on‑ramp and off‑ramp activities.  
  * Document model governance, data‑quality checks, and human‑oversight procedures.  

- **Update cross‑border compliance policies**  
  * Review contracts and data‑sharing agreements with European partners for clauses that address the SEC‑AMF information‑exchange protocol.  
  * Design a dual‑jurisdiction incident‑response plan that triggers reporting to both regulators within the required timeframes.  

- **Prepare for potential IPO and crowdfunding rule changes**  
  * Conduct a gap analysis of current prospectus and offering‑memorandum templates against the advisory committee’s discussion points (e.g., enhanced risk‑factor disclosures, investor‑qualification verification).  
  * Align internal fundraising workflows with the SEC’s “modernization” goals—consider adopting electronic filing tools that can adapt quickly to rule updates.  

- **Enhance cross‑trading oversight**  
  * Implement automated alerts that flag any trade between a registered fund and an affiliate involving newly eligible securities.  
  * Ensure that the trade‑approval workflow captures fairness analysis, market‑price verification, and a written conflict‑of‑interest disclosure for each transaction.  

- **Strengthen fiduciary‑duty controls**  
  * Institute a “dual‑sign‑off” requirement for any internal reallocation of client assets that could benefit the firm or its principals.  
  * Keep a detailed audit trail of all portfolio adjustments, including the rationale, the client’s consent status, and the timing relative to market movements.
