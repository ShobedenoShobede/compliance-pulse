**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils FY 2027 Examination Priorities, Expands Cross‑Trading Rules, and Pushes IPO & Crowdfunding Modernisation – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track Right Now  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released a flurry of announcements this week that together reshape the regulatory landscape for fintech firms, investment advisers, and fund managers.  

1. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The SEC’s Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year‑2027 focus areas. The report highlights heightened scrutiny of:  
   * Market‑making and liquidity provision by broker‑dealers, especially those using algorithmic or high‑frequency strategies.  
   * Cyber‑security and data‑privacy controls across all SEC‑registered entities.  
   * Environmental, social and governance (ESG) disclosures, with a particular eye on green‑bond and impact‑investment claims.  
   * Anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and sanctions compliance, including the use of “virtual‑asset service providers” (VASPs).  

2. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a memorandum of understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and Europe. The MOU covers joint examinations, coordinated enforcement actions, and the exchange of supervisory findings.  

3. **Small Business Advisory Committee on IPO Modernisation & Crowdfunding** – The Committee met on Oct. 27 to discuss updates to the IPO process and the Regulation Crowdfunding framework. Topics included simplifying the “test‑the‑waters” approach, expanding the eligibility of “qualified investors,” and clarifying the use of digital platforms for filing and disclosure.  

4. **Proposed Expansion of Cross‑Trading Rules** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would allow registered funds to trade a broader class of securities with affiliates, provided the trades are “fair and reasonable” and meet new transparency and reporting thresholds.  

5. **Final Judgment in the Western Asset Cherry‑Picking Case** – The SEC moved for a final judgment against former Western Asset co‑CIO Stephen Kenneth Leech II for “cherry‑picking” – the illegal practice of directing profitable trades to the firm while leaving less‑favourable trades to client accounts. The judgment reinforces the SEC’s zero‑tolerance stance on front‑running and fiduciary breaches.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Examination priorities set the compliance agenda.**  
The SEC’s FY 2027 focus signals where examiners will look first. Fintech platforms that host algorithmic trading, provide digital brokerage services, or manage ESG‑linked products must be ready for deeper on‑site reviews. Cyber‑security expectations are now explicit; any breach or inadequate controls could trigger enforcement. For firms that process virtual‑asset transactions, the AML emphasis means existing BSA/AML programs must cover crypto‑related activities, including customer‑due‑diligence on wallet addresses and transaction monitoring for sanctioned parties.  

**2. Cross‑border coordination raises the stakes for multinational fintechs.**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU creates a formal channel for the two regulators to share data and conduct joint examinations. Companies that market advisory services or investment products to both U.S. and French investors can no longer assume a “regulatory island” approach. Inconsistent documentation, divergent reporting timelines, or gaps in client disclosures could attract simultaneous scrutiny from both agencies, amplifying enforcement risk.  

**3. IPO and crowdfunding reforms affect capital‑raising strategies.**  
The Small Business Advisory Committee’s discussion points hint at a more streamlined, technology‑friendly IPO pathway and a broader investor base for Regulation Crowdfunding. Fintech firms planning a public offering or a crowdfunded raise should monitor forthcoming rule changes. Early adoption of the recommended digital filing formats and investor‑verification tools can reduce the time and cost of capital formation and position firms as compliant innovators.  

**4. Expanded cross‑trading rules could change fund‑affiliate transactions.**  
If adopted, the rule changes will let registered funds trade a wider range of securities with affiliates, but only if the trades meet stricter fairness and reporting standards. Fintech fund‑administration platforms that automate affiliate trades must update their pricing models, audit trails, and disclosure templates to meet the new “fair and reasonable” test. Failure to do so could result in violations of Section 2(b)(13) of the Investment Company Act.  

**5. The cherry‑picking judgment reinforces fiduciary duties.**  
The Western Asset case serves as a reminder that any preferential treatment of a firm’s own accounts over client accounts is prohibited. Fintechs that operate “managed accounts,” robo‑advisors, or any algorithmic order‑routing system must ensure that trade execution logic is transparent, auditable, and free from hidden profit‑shifting. The judgment also signals that the SEC will pursue civil penalties and disgorgement where it finds evidence of systematic abuse.  

Overall, these developments tighten the regulatory net around technology‑driven finance, demand higher data integrity, and push firms toward more transparent, investor‑friendly practices. Non‑compliance will not only invite enforcement but also erode trust with customers and partners.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map examiner focus to your risk registers.**  
  * Review your internal audit plans and ensure that market‑making, cyber‑security, ESG disclosures, and AML controls are each covered with documented testing procedures.  

- **Upgrade crypto AML controls now.**  
  * Integrate sanctions‑screening of wallet addresses, enhance transaction monitoring for rapid token swaps, and retain detailed logs for at least five years to satisfy both BSA and upcoming SEC expectations.  

- **Align U.S. and French reporting processes.**  
  * Conduct a gap analysis of client onboarding, KYC, and disclosure documents used in the U.S. versus France. Standardize data fields and establish a shared secure portal for regulator‑requested information exchange.  

- **Prepare for IPO/crowdfunding rule changes.**  
  * Pilot the SEC’s “test‑the‑waters” digital workflow on a small offering to identify bottlenecks. Update your investor‑qualification questionnaire to capture the expanded “qualified investor” criteria that the Committee is likely to adopt.
