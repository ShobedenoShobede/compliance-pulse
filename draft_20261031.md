**Headline**  
SEC Clears Path for New Repo Flexibility, Sets FY 2027 Exam Focus, Expands Cross‑Trading, and Boosts International Adviser Cooperation – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Track Right Now  

---  

### What Happened  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements on October 31, 2026 that together reshape several compliance frontiers for firms that trade Treasury securities, manage investment funds, or provide advisory services through technology platforms.  

1. **Exemptive relief for certain repo trades** – The SEC granted an exemption that frees broker‑dealers and other participants from the mandatory central‑clearing rule for specific inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions involving U.S. Treasury securities. The relief applies when the trade is between affiliates of the same firm or when the counter‑party is located outside the United States, provided the parties meet the conditions outlined in the order.  

2. **Fiscal Year 2027 examination priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its FY 2027 priority list. The top areas of focus include: (a) the effectiveness of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) programs; (b) the adequacy of cybersecurity controls; (c) the handling of “non‑public information” in market‑making and algorithmic trading; and (d) the implementation of the SEC’s recent rules on “investment adviser disclosure” and “fund cross‑trading.”  

3. **MOU with France’s AMF** – The SEC and the Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing and joint supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France. The agreement covers routine data exchanges, coordinated investigations, and joint guidance on emerging risks such as digital‑asset advice.  

4. **Small Business Advisory Committee meeting on IPO modernization** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on October 27, 2026 to discuss updates to the IPO process and possible changes to Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying the filing process for emerging companies and adjusting the “testing‑the‑waters” rules that let issuers gauge investor interest before a formal offering.  

5. **Proposed rule change to expand cross‑trading** – The agency proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would allow registered funds to trade a broader class of securities with affiliates, provided the trades meet new transparency and pricing safeguards. The proposal also calls for enhanced reporting of such trades to the SEC.

### Why It Matters  

**Repo exemption reshapes clearing risk** – Central clearing has been the default for Treasury repo trades since the 2022 rule aimed at reducing systemic risk. By carving out an exemption for inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. counterparties, the SEC acknowledges that some market participants—especially those using proprietary platforms to manage internal liquidity—face higher costs and operational burdens when forced into a clearing house. Fintech firms that run automated repo desks or provide repo‑matching services must now reassess whether their trades qualify for the exemption and adjust their clearing‑risk models accordingly. Failure to apply the exemption correctly could expose a firm to unnecessary clearing fees or, conversely, to regulatory scrutiny if an ineligible trade is mistakenly treated as exempt.  

**FY 2027 exam focus hits core compliance pillars** – The examination priorities signal where the SEC will direct its limited inspection resources. AML and cybersecurity have long been high‑risk areas, but the explicit inclusion of “non‑public information” handling in algorithmic and market‑making contexts raises the bar for firms that use AI‑driven trading engines. Fintech platforms that ingest market data, execute orders, or provide advisory analytics must ensure that any material, non‑public information is segregated, logged, and only used where permitted. The emphasis on recent adviser‑disclosure rules also means that any fintech‑enabled advisory service—robo‑advisors, hybrid platforms, or third‑party data aggregators—needs to verify that its client‑facing disclosures meet the latest standards.  

**Cross‑border MOU tightens oversight of dual‑jurisdiction advisers** – The SEC‑AMF MOU creates a formal channel for sharing information about firms that are registered in the United States but also serve French investors, and vice‑versa. For fintech companies that power multi‑jurisdictional advisory portals, this means a higher likelihood that any compliance deficiency identified in France could be communicated to U.S. regulators quickly. The MOU also paves the way for joint examinations, so firms should anticipate coordinated requests for records that span both regulatory regimes.  

**IPO modernization signals a shift for early‑stage issuers** – The advisory committee’s upcoming discussion on IPO modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding could lead to a streamlined filing process and revised fundraising caps. Fintech platforms that host equity‑crowdfunding campaigns or provide IPO preparation tools need to monitor the outcome closely. Even if the final rules are modest, any change that lowers the cost or complexity of going public will likely increase the volume of issuers using technology‑driven services, expanding the compliance footprint for those platforms.  

**Expanded cross‑trading rules affect fund‑management tech** – The proposed amendment widens the types of securities that registered funds can trade with affiliates, but it also imposes stricter pricing and reporting requirements. Fintech solutions that automate fund‑to‑affiliate trades—such as portfolio‑rebalancing engines or internal liquidity‑management modules—must be updated to capture the new data fields and generate the required disclosures. Missing a reporting deadline or failing to demonstrate that a trade was at “fair market value” could trigger enforcement action and damage a firm’s reputation with investors.

### What to Do  

- **Review repo workflows for exemption eligibility** – Map every repo transaction against the new exemption criteria. Update your trade‑capture system to flag qualifying inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. trades and route them away from the clearing‑house workflow. Document the rationale for each exemption decision in case of an audit.  

- **Strengthen controls around non‑public information** – Conduct a gap analysis of how your algorithms ingest, store, and act on market data. Implement audit logs that capture who accessed material non‑public information, when, and for what purpose. Ensure that any AI model that uses such data is restricted to permitted uses under the upcoming exam focus.
