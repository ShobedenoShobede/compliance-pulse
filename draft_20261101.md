**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out five back‑to‑back moves – repo‑clearing exemption, FY‑27 exam focus, U.S.–France adviser MOU, IPO‑crowdfunding round‑table, and a cross‑trading rule tweak – that fintech firms must size up now.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**1. Repo‑clearing exemption** – The SEC granted limited exemptive relief from the Treasury‑securities central‑clearing mandate for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions. The relief applies when a broker‑dealer, its affiliates, or a foreign‑based repo counter‑party conducts a repo that is fully collateralised with U.S. Treasuries, but the transaction does not pass through a registered clearing agency. The exemption is temporary, subject to a reporting requirement, and will be reviewed after the Treasury’s broader clearing deadline passes.

**2. FY 2027 examination priorities** – The Division of Examinations released its fiscal‑year‑2027 priority list. The top focus areas are: (a) compliance with the new Treasury‑clearing rules; (b) oversight of fintech platforms that facilitate securities‑based crowdfunding and digital‑asset offerings; (c) anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and cyber‑risk controls for cloud‑based trading systems; (d) supervision of “cross‑trading” activities by registered investment funds; and (e) evaluation of broker‑dealer governance around “inter‑affiliate” transactions.

**3. U.S.–France supervisory MOU** – The SEC and the Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers joint investigations, coordinated supervisory actions, and the exchange of findings on misconduct involving U.S. and French clients.

**4. Small‑Business Advisory Committee meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for 27 Oct 2026 to discuss two agenda items: (a) modernizing the IPO process for emerging growth companies, and (b) potential regulatory adjustments to Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) to make it more attractive for fintech platforms and small issuers.

**5. Proposed cross‑trading rule expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the list of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliates, provided the transaction is at fair market value, documented, and does not disadvantage other shareholders. The proposal also clarifies reporting obligations and introduces a “single‑transaction” exception for low‑volume trades.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑clearing exemption – immediate operational relief, but a compliance watch‑point**  
Fintech firms that run repo desks or provide repo‑matching services can now execute certain inter‑affiliate or foreign‑counterparty repos without routing them through a clearinghouse. This reduces settlement friction and lowers costs for clients who need short‑term Treasury funding. However, the exemption is conditional: firms must file quarterly reports on exempted repos, retain detailed transaction records, and be ready to revert to mandatory clearing if the SEC rescinds the relief. Failure to track exempted repos accurately could trigger enforcement findings under the SEC’s FY 2027 exam focus on Treasury‑clearing compliance.

**FY 2027 exam priorities – a roadmap for regulators**  
The exam priorities act as a pre‑emptive checklist. The SEC will likely target fintech platforms that host securities‑crowdfunding portals, cloud‑based trading engines, or that facilitate cross‑trading between funds and affiliates. Companies that have not yet mapped their AML, cyber‑security, and governance controls to the new focus areas could face heightened scrutiny, higher examination fees, or remedial orders. Aligning internal risk‑assessment cycles with these priorities now can reduce the surprise factor when exam teams arrive.

**U.S.–France MOU – cross‑border advisory risk escalates**  
Fintech advisers that serve both U.S. and French clients must anticipate more coordinated supervision. The MOU means that a breach identified by the AMF—such as inadequate suitability testing or a conflict‑of‑interest disclosure failure—could be shared with the SEC and result in a joint enforcement action. Firms should review their client‑onboarding and reporting processes for French‑related activities, ensuring that data protection, suitability, and fee‑disclosure standards meet both regulators’ expectations.

**IPO modernization & Reg CF discussion – platform opportunity and regulatory shift**  
The advisory committee’s upcoming discussion signals that the SEC is still open to tweaking the IPO pipeline and the crowdfunding regime. For fintech platforms that enable Regulation Crowdfunding, any change—whether it’s a higher offering limit, streamlined disclosure, or a new “tier‑2” crowdfunding category—could expand the addressable market. Conversely, a move toward stricter disclosure or investor‑qualification rules could raise compliance costs. Keeping tabs on the committee’s recommendations will help product teams anticipate feature updates and adjust risk‑management frameworks.

**Cross‑trading rule expansion – new trading latitude, but tighter documentation**  
If the SEC adopts the proposed amendments, registered funds can trade a broader set of securities with their affiliates, potentially improving portfolio liquidity and reducing transaction costs. However, the rule adds documentation requirements: each trade must be priced at fair market value, recorded in a central register, and reported to the SEC within a set timeframe. Fintech vendors that provide fund‑management software or trade‑execution services will need to update their systems to capture the additional data fields and generate the required reports.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map exempted repo activity** – Create a live inventory of all inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repo trades. Tag each transaction with its exemption status, attach supporting collateral documentation, and set up automated quarterly reporting to the SEC.  

- **Align internal audit with FY 2027 priorities** – Add a dedicated line item to your annual audit plan for each of the five focus areas (Treasury‑clearing, crowdfunding platforms, AML/cyber controls, cross‑trading, and inter‑affiliate governance). Conduct a pre‑exam self‑assessment and remediate gaps before the regulator’s exam window opens.  

- **Review cross‑border advisory processes** – Conduct a gap analysis of all U.S.–France client relationships. Verify that suitability questionnaires, fee disclosures, and data‑privacy notices satisfy both SEC and AMF expectations. Document a joint‑supervision response plan in case the AMF shares findings with the SEC.  

- **Monitor the Small‑Business Advisory Committee outcomes** – Assign a product‑owner to track the meeting minutes and any subsequent rule proposals. Model the impact of possible Reg CF changes (e.g., higher offering caps) on your platform
