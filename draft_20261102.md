**Headline**  
SEC rolls out five key moves – repo‑clearing relief, FY‑27 exam focus, US‑France adviser MOU, IPO‑crowdfunding hearing, and broader cross‑trading rules – that will reshape compliance work for fintech firms and their partners.  

---  

### What Happened  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items today.  

1. **Repo‑Clearing Exemption** – The SEC granted limited exemptive relief from the Treasury‑clearing rule for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions. The relief applies to broker‑dealers and other market participants that are moving toward central clearing of U.S. Treasury securities, but it carves out a narrow set of repo trades that can continue under the old bilateral model.  

2. **FY‑2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year‑2027 priorities. The list spotlights three high‑risk areas: (a) the use of third‑party service providers, especially cloud and data‑analytics platforms; (b) the integrity of capital‑raising mechanisms such as Regulation Crowdfunding and Regulation A+; and (c) the oversight of “digital‑asset” activities that intersect with securities law.  

3. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC signed a memorandum of understanding with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The MOU creates a formal channel for information‑sharing and joint supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France.  

4. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss two topics: modernizing the IPO process for emerging companies and reviewing possible tweaks to Regulation Crowdfunding. The committee will hear from industry participants and may forward recommendations to the Commission.  

5. **Cross‑Trading Rule Proposal** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the class of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliates, provided the trades meet transparency, pricing, and best‑interest standards. The proposal is open for public comment through Dec. 31, 2026.

### Why It Matters  

**Repo‑Clearing Relief** – Fintech platforms that facilitate repo financing for institutional clients often rely on inter‑affiliate structures to manage liquidity. The exemption means those platforms can keep using existing bilateral arrangements for a subset of trades while the broader industry transitions to central clearing. However, the exemption is limited to “certain” inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repos, so firms must verify whether their specific transaction types qualify. Misclassifying a repo could trigger a violation of the Treasury‑clearing rule and expose the firm to enforcement risk.  

**FY‑27 Exam Focus** – The examination priorities give a clear signal of where SEC examiners will look first. Third‑party service providers—especially cloud‑based data‑analytics tools that fintech firms use for risk scoring, KYC, and trade monitoring—are now a top‑risk area. Firms must be able to demonstrate robust vendor‑due‑diligence, contractual controls, and incident‑response plans. The emphasis on capital‑raising mechanisms means that any fintech that runs a crowdfunding portal, Reg A+ offering, or tokenized securities platform should expect heightened scrutiny of investor‑protection safeguards, disclosure practices, and anti‑money‑laundering (AML) controls. Finally, the mention of digital‑asset activities signals that any overlap between crypto‑related services and securities law will be examined closely; firms should be ready to show how they apply the “investment contract” test and maintain proper registration or exemption documentation.  

**SEC‑AMF MOU** – For fintech firms that serve U.S. and French clients—whether through advisory services, fund distribution, or data‑processing—this MOU creates a joint supervisory pathway. Information that was once siloed in each regulator’s system can now be shared, potentially accelerating cross‑border investigations. Companies must therefore ensure that their data‑privacy and record‑keeping practices meet the standards of both regulators, as inconsistencies could be flagged in a joint review.  

**IPO‑Crowdfunding Advisory Meeting** – The upcoming advisory committee meeting is a barometer for upcoming rule changes. If the SEC moves to modernize IPO processes—perhaps by allowing more digital filing, real‑time pricing data, or expanded use of electronic signatures—fintech platforms that provide IPO‑related services (e.g., electronic prospectus distribution, investor onboarding) will need to adapt quickly. Likewise, any tweaks to Regulation Crowdfunding could affect the permissible fundraising limits, investor‑accreditation thresholds, or reporting obligations, all of which directly impact fintech crowdfunding portals.  

**Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – The proposed rule change could open new liquidity channels for registered funds that already have affiliate relationships with fintech‑enabled marketplaces or secondary‑trading platforms. By allowing a broader set of securities to be cross‑traded, the SEC is effectively encouraging more intra‑group activity, but only if firms can prove that pricing is fair and that trades are transparent to investors. Fintech firms that act as intermediaries in such affiliate trades must be prepared to capture and report detailed pricing data, maintain audit trails, and implement conflict‑of‑interest safeguards. Failure to meet the new standards could result in enforcement actions or investor complaints.  

Overall, the five updates intersect around three themes: (1) **greater scrutiny of third‑party and cross‑border arrangements**, (2) **a push toward more transparent and centralized market infrastructure**, and (3) **the SEC’s willingness to modernize rules that affect emerging fintech business models.** Compliance teams that treat these themes as a single compliance roadmap will be better positioned to avoid surprises and to leverage the regulatory shifts for competitive advantage.

- **Map Your Repo Activity** – Inventory all repo transactions, flag those that are inter‑affiliate or involve non‑U.S. counterparties, and confirm whether they fall within the new exemption. Update your internal repo‑clearing policy and train trading desks on the eligibility criteria.
