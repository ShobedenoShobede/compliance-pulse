**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out five key moves—repo‑clearing relief, FY‑27 exam focus, U.S.–France adviser MOU, IPO‑crowdfunding forum, and wider cross‑trading for funds—forcing fintech compliance teams to adjust policies, data feeds, and risk controls now.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**1. Repo‑clearing exemption** – The SEC issued an exemptive order that frees broker‑dealers and other market participants from the mandatory central‑clearing requirement for certain Treasury repo trades that are conducted between affiliates of the same firm or with non‑U.S. counterparties. The relief applies only to “inter‑affiliate” and “non‑U.S.” repo transactions that meet the SEC’s narrow criteria, and it is limited to a three‑year window pending a final rulemaking.

**2. FY‑2027 examination priorities** – The Division of Examinations released its fiscal‑year 2027 priorities. The list highlights four focus areas: (a) the implementation of the SEC’s 2024 “Fundamental Review of the Trading Platform” rules, (b) the use of artificial‑intelligence tools in investment‑advice and order‑routing, (c) the integrity of market data and cyber‑security safeguards, and (d) the oversight of “crowdfunding” portals and related capital‑formation activities.

**3. U.S.–France supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve cooperation on the supervision of investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The MOU establishes a framework for sharing information, coordinating investigations, and aligning supervisory expectations for firms that serve U.S. and French investors.

**4. Small‑Business Advisory Committee meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss two agenda items: (a) modernization of the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) registration process, and (b) possible rule changes to Regulation Crowdfunding that could expand the amount of capital that start‑ups can raise and simplify the filing burden on issuers.

**5. Proposed cross‑trading expansion** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. Currently, only a limited set of securities may be traded between a registered fund and its affiliates. The proposal would broaden the list of eligible securities, relax certain pricing constraints, and require additional disclosures to investors about the nature of the affiliate transactions.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑clearing relief** – Fintech platforms that automate repo financing for broker‑dealers often embed the central‑clearing requirement into their transaction‑processing logic. The new exemption means those platforms must now support a parallel “non‑cleared” workflow for qualifying trades. Failure to separate the two streams could lead to inadvertent rule breaches, inaccurate reporting to the SEC, and unnecessary clearing fees. Moreover, the three‑year “temporary” status signals that the SEC may later require a permanent rule change, so firms should design systems that can be toggled on or off without major re‑coding.

**Examination priorities** – The FY‑27 focus areas intersect directly with fintech operations. AI‑driven advice engines and automated order‑routing tools will be scrutinized for bias, model‑risk management, and explainability. The emphasis on market‑data integrity means that any data‑aggregation service used by a fintech must demonstrate robust provenance, timestamping, and cyber‑security controls. Finally, the heightened attention on crowdfunding portals signals that regulators expect tighter KYC/AML checks and clearer disclosure of fees and risks for retail investors using these platforms.

**U.S.–France MOU** – Many fintech firms now serve both U.S. and European customers through a single technology stack. The MOU creates a formal channel for the SEC and AMF to exchange supervisory findings, which could result in coordinated enforcement actions. Companies that previously treated U.S. and French compliance as separate silos must now align policies on adviser registration, client suitability, and data‑privacy to satisfy both regulators simultaneously.

**IPO‑crowdfunding discussion** – Potential rule changes could raise the maximum amount a company may raise via Regulation Crowdfunding and simplify the filing process. For fintech platforms that host equity‑crowdfunding campaigns, this could mean a surge in deal flow and new product opportunities. However, it also raises the stakes for due‑diligence, investor‑education, and post‑offering reporting. Early preparation will help firms capture the upside while staying clear of future compliance gaps.

**Cross‑trading expansion** – By widening the securities that funds can trade with affiliates, the SEC is encouraging greater internal liquidity but also increasing the risk of conflicts of interest. Fintech solutions that manage fund portfolios will need to incorporate new eligibility checks, pricing‑fairness algorithms, and enhanced investor‑disclosure modules. Ignoring the proposed changes could expose funds to shareholder lawsuits or regulator‑issued cease‑and‑desist orders.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Update repo‑processing rules** – Add a flag in your repo‑trade engine to identify “inter‑affiliate” and “non‑U.S.” transactions. Build a parallel workflow that bypasses the clearing step, and set up automated alerts to review any trade that falls outside the exemption criteria.

- **Audit AI and data‑feed controls** – Conduct a quick gap analysis against the FY‑27 priorities: verify model documentation, bias‑testing logs, and data‑source certifications. Document cyber‑security measures for market‑data APIs and be ready to produce them in an examination.

- **Synchronize U.S. and French compliance policies** – Map your adviser registration, client‑onboarding, and privacy procedures against both SEC and AMF rules. Establish a single “cross‑border compliance” team that can respond to information‑sharing requests from either regulator.

- **Prep for crowdfunding rule changes** – Review your platform’s KYC/AML workflow, investor‑education content, and post‑offering reporting templates. Draft a “future‑state” process that can handle higher fundraising caps and fewer filing steps, and test it with a pilot offering.

- **Enhance cross‑trading safeguards** – Modify your fund‑management software to automatically check the expanded securities list, enforce the new pricing methodology, and generate the required disclosures. Run a mock‑trade scenario to confirm that the system flags any affiliate transaction that could be perceived as unfair.

By taking these steps now, fintech compliance teams can turn today’s regulatory announcements into a competitive advantage—ensuring smooth operations, avoiding enforcement surprises, and positioning their platforms
