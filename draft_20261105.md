**HEADLINE**  
SEC Rolls Out Five Key Moves – Repo Relief, FY‑27 Exam Focus, France MOU, Crowdfunding Talk, and Broader Cross‑Trading Rules – All With Immediate Impact on Fintech Compliance  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**1. Repo‑clearing exemption** – The SEC issued an exemptive order that frees certain inter‑affiliate repo trades and repo transactions involving non‑U.S. counterparties from the mandatory Treasury‑clearing requirements that have been rolling out over the past few years. The relief applies only to repo transactions that are fully collateralized by U.S. Treasury securities, are executed between affiliates of the same broker‑dealer or clearing member, or involve a foreign dealer that meets the SEC’s “non‑U.S. repo” criteria. The order is effective immediately and will remain in place until the SEC decides otherwise.  

**2. FY 2027 examination priorities** – The Division of Examinations released its fiscal‑year‑2027 priority list. The top focus areas are: (a) compliance with the new Treasury‑clearing rules, (b) oversight of fintech platforms that facilitate securities‑based crowdfunding and digital‑asset offerings, (c) anti‑money‑laundering (AML) controls around high‑frequency trading and automated market‑making, (d) governance of “non‑bank” custodial services, and (e) risk management for “inter‑affiliate” financing structures, including repo and securities lending. The SEC says it will conduct more “targeted” examinations rather than broad, schedule‑driven reviews.  

**3. U.S.–France supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers joint monitoring of cross‑border advisory activities, coordinated responses to misconduct, and a streamlined process for sharing supervisory findings.  

**4. Small‑business advisory meeting on IPO modernization** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on 27 October 2026 to discuss two items: (a) potential changes to the IPO registration process that could speed up filings for emerging companies, and (b) possible regulatory tweaks to Regulation Crowdfunding that would broaden the pool of eligible investors and increase fundraising caps. The meeting is open to public comment, and the SEC has invited fintech firms that provide capital‑raising platforms to submit observations.  

**5. Proposed expansion of cross‑trading eligibility** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. Currently, only a narrow set of securities may be traded between a registered fund and its affiliates. The proposal would add most listed equity securities, certain corporate bonds, and a limited set of exchange‑traded funds (ETFs) to the eligible list, provided the transaction is “fair‑valued” and disclosed to fund shareholders. The comment period runs for 60 days, ending 30 December 2026.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑clearing exemption** – Fintech firms that operate as broker‑dealers, clearing members, or repo platforms often structure intra‑group financing to reduce operational costs. The exemption removes the need to route those specific repo trades through a central Treasury clearinghouse, which can lower settlement fees and simplify back‑office processing. However, the relief is limited to “inter‑affiliate” or “non‑U.S.” deals, so firms must be able to identify and document when a trade qualifies. Failure to do so could trigger a breach of the broader Treasury‑clearing mandate and expose the firm to enforcement risk.  

**FY 2027 exam focus** – The examination priority list tells compliance teams exactly where the SEC will look first. The emphasis on Treasury‑clearing, fintech crowdfunding, AML for automated trading, and inter‑affiliate financing means that any gaps in policies, data‑capture, or reporting in these areas could lead to a targeted examination. Because the SEC plans to conduct “targeted” reviews, a single weak spot may invite a deep dive that expands to related functions.  

**U.S.–France MOU** – Many fintech platforms now serve both U.S. and European clients. The new MOU means that supervisory findings in France can be shared with the SEC and vice‑versa. A compliance breach flagged by the AMF—such as a mis‑sale of a European‑registered fund to U.S. investors—could quickly become a U.S. enforcement matter. Firms must therefore harmonize their cross‑border advisory and custody controls, and be prepared to respond to foreign regulator requests.  

**Crowdfunding and IPO modernization discussion** – If the SEC adopts the advisory committee’s recommendations, the IPO filing process could become faster and less costly, and Regulation Crowdfunding could see higher fundraising limits and broader investor eligibility. Fintech platforms that host equity‑crowdfunding or assist companies with direct listings will need to adjust their onboarding, disclosure, and investor‑verification workflows to accommodate any new thresholds or reporting requirements. Early engagement in the comment process can shape rules that affect platform revenue models.  

**Cross‑trading rule expansion** – Allowing a wider range of securities to be cross‑traded between funds and affiliates could open new arbitrage and liquidity‑management opportunities for fintech‑driven asset‑management services. At the same time, it raises compliance demands: firms must ensure fair‑value pricing, maintain transparent audit trails, and disclose the trades to fund shareholders in a timely manner. The proposed rule also expands the scope of “affiliated party” transactions that must be reported under Form N-CSR and Form N‑PORT, increasing data‑collection burdens.  

Overall, these five actions create a tighter regulatory net around the areas where fintech firms are most active—repo financing, cross‑border advisory, digital capital formation, and fund‑level trading. Ignoring any of them could result in enforcement notices, fines, or reputational damage.
