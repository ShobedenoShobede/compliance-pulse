**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s 2026 Action Pack: New Repo‑Clearing Relief, FY‑27 Exam Priorities, Cross‑Trading Rules, France MOU, and Small‑Biz IPO Talk – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Know Today  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**1. Exemptive relief for inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repo trades**  
The SEC issued an exemption that frees certain broker‑dealers and other market participants from the mandatory central‑clearing requirement for U.S. Treasury repos when the transaction is between affiliates of the same firm or involves a non‑U.S. counter‑party. The relief is limited to “inter‑affiliate” repos and to repos that are not cleared through a U.S. clearinghouse, provided the parties meet the SEC’s eligibility criteria and maintain adequate risk controls.  

**2. FY‑2027 examination priorities released**  
The Division of Examinations published its focus areas for fiscal year 2027. Top priorities include: (a) the effectiveness of firms’ anti‑money‑laundering (AML) programs, (b) the implementation of the SEC’s “Regulation S‑X” reporting enhancements for digital assets, (c) the oversight of third‑party service providers, especially cloud and fintech vendors, and (d) the adequacy of internal controls around market‑making and liquidity provision in the wake of recent volatility.  

**3. SEC‑AMF supervisory memorandum of understanding**  
The U.S. SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed an MOU to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The agreement covers joint investigations, coordinated supervisory reviews, and the exchange of supervisory findings related to cross‑border activities, including fintech platforms that offer advisory or execution services to U.S. and French clients.  

**4. Small Business Advisory Committee meeting on IPO modernization**  
The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss proposals that would modernize the IPO process and expand Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying the registration process for “mini‑IPOs,” lowering the cost of filing, and expanding the investor base for early‑stage fintech firms.  

**5. Proposed rule change to expand cross‑trading eligibility for registered funds**  
The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. Currently, only a narrow set of securities may be traded between a registered fund and its affiliates. The proposal would broaden the list to include additional fixed‑income instruments and certain equity securities, provided the trade meets pricing and disclosure safeguards. The public comment period runs for 60 days.

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑clearing exemption reshapes treasury‑repo workflows**  
Fintech platforms that facilitate repo financing for institutional clients often route inter‑affiliate trades through their own clearing infrastructure to reduce latency and cost. The new exemption removes the need to connect those trades to a U.S. clearinghouse, potentially lowering operational expenses and simplifying settlement. However, firms must still demonstrate robust collateral‑valuation and counter‑party‑risk controls. Failure to meet the SEC’s criteria could expose the firm to enforcement if a non‑cleared repo defaults.  

**FY‑27 exam priorities signal where regulators will look first**  
The emphasis on AML, digital‑asset reporting, and third‑party risk means compliance teams should audit their current controls now, not wait for an examiner’s visit. Fintech firms that rely on cloud providers, API aggregators, or outsourced data‑analytics services must be prepared to show documented due‑diligence, contract terms that allocate supervisory responsibility, and real‑time monitoring of suspicious activity. The focus on “Regulation S‑X” signals that even firms with modest digital‑asset exposure need a reporting framework that captures transaction‑level data, timestamps, and counter‑party identifiers.  

**SEC‑AMF MOU expands cross‑border supervisory reach**  
Fintech companies that serve both U.S. and French investors—or that partner with French advisers—will now be subject to coordinated oversight. Information shared between the SEC and AMF could trigger joint examinations, and any deficiencies identified by the AMF may be reported back to the SEC. This raises the stakes for maintaining consistent compliance policies across jurisdictions, especially around client onboarding, suitability assessments, and data‑privacy obligations under GDPR and the U.S. privacy framework.  

**Small‑biz IPO discussion hints at a faster path to public markets**  
If the SEC adopts the committee’s recommendations, fintech startups could launch public offerings with fewer disclosure burdens and lower filing fees. That would make the IPO route more attractive compared with Regulation Crowdfunding or private placements. Companies should begin mapping the potential impact on their capital‑raising strategies, including the need to upgrade investor‑relations capabilities, expand reporting infrastructure, and prepare for ongoing public‑company obligations.  

**Cross‑trading rule change could affect fund‑of‑funds and platform‑based fund structures**  
Many fintech platforms act as “wrapper” vehicles that hold multiple registered funds and execute intra‑platform trades to manage liquidity. Expanding the list of eligible securities will give these platforms more flexibility to rebalance portfolios without triggering external market trades, potentially improving execution quality and reducing costs. However, the proposed safeguards—fair‑price testing, transparent disclosure to fund shareholders, and enhanced record‑keeping—will require updates to trade‑execution policies and systems that track intra‑platform activity.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review repo‑clearing exemption eligibility**  
  - Verify that any inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo transactions your platform supports meet the SEC’s definition of “eligible.”  
  - Document collateral‑valuation methods, margin requirements, and settlement procedures.  
  - Update internal policies to reflect the exemption and train operations staff on the new limits.

- **Conduct a pre‑exam readiness check for FY‑27 priorities**  
  - Run a gap analysis on AML controls, focusing on transaction monitoring thresholds for digital‑asset trades.  
  - Map all third‑party service providers (cloud, data, API) and confirm that contracts include supervisory clauses and data‑security standards.  
  - Build a reporting template that captures the data points required by the forthcoming “Regulation S‑X” rules for digital assets.

- **Align cross‑border compliance with the SEC‑AMF MOU**  
  - Perform a jurisdictional risk assessment for any French‑based advisers, investors, or counterparties.
