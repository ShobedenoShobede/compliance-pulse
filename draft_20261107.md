**HEADLINE**  
SEC Rolls Out Five Major Moves – Repo‑Clearing Relief, FY‑27 Exam Focus, US‑France MOU, IPO‑Crowdfunding Talk, and Expanded Cross‑Trading Rules  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission announced five separate actions today that could reshape how fintech firms, broker‑dealers, and investment advisers operate.

1. **Repo‑Clearing Exemptions** – The SEC issued an exemptive order that frees certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions from the mandatory central‑clearing requirement for Treasury securities. The relief applies when the repo is executed between affiliates of the same ultimate parent or when the counterparty is a non‑U.S. entity that does not have a clearing obligation under U.S. law. The order is limited to “eligible” repos that meet specific documentation and risk‑management standards.

2. **FY‑27 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations released its fiscal‑year‑2027 examination agenda. The top priorities include: (a) enforcement of the new “modernized” IPO rules, (b) scrutiny of Regulation Crowdfunding offerings, (c) assessment of cyber‑risk controls in fintech platforms, (d) review of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) programs for digital‑asset intermediaries, and (e) evaluation of compliance with the SEC’s recent cross‑trading proposal for registered funds.

3. **US‑France Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve cooperation on investment advisers and investment‑service providers. The MOU creates a formal channel for information‑sharing, joint investigations, and coordinated supervisory actions on cross‑border activities that involve U.S. and French investors.

4. **Small Business Advisory Committee on IPO Modernization & Crowdfunding** – The Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss the SEC’s ongoing work to modernize the IPO process and to evaluate possible rule changes to Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying prospectus requirements, expanding the use of electronic “road‑shows,” and adjusting the $5 million annual fundraising cap for crowdfunding portals.

5. **Proposed Expansion of Cross‑Trading Rules** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the list of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliated entities, provided the trades are at fair market value, documented, and do not disadvantage other shareholders. The proposal also adds new reporting requirements for such transactions.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑Clearing Relief** – Many fintech platforms that offer short‑term financing or liquidity‑management services rely on repo transactions to move cash quickly. The new exemption reduces the operational burden of clearing through a central counterparty for a sizable slice of repo activity—especially when the parties are within the same corporate family or are foreign subsidiaries. Companies can now keep those repos “off‑exchange” without risking a breach of the Treasury‑clearing rule, but they must still meet the SEC’s documentation and risk‑management thresholds. Failure to adhere could trigger enforcement actions or force a costly migration to a clearing house.

**FY‑27 Examination Priorities** – The examination roadmap signals where the SEC will focus its resources. Fintech firms that run IPO‑related services, host Regulation Crowdfunding portals, or provide digital‑asset custody must expect deeper scrutiny. The inclusion of cyber‑risk and AML for digital‑asset intermediaries means that any gaps in security controls, incident‑response plans, or transaction‑monitoring could become examination triggers. Early alignment with these priorities helps avoid surprise findings and potential penalties.

**US‑France MOU** – Cross‑border advisers and service providers that serve both U.S. and French clients now face a tighter supervisory net. Information shared under the MOU can accelerate investigations, so firms must ensure that their compliance data (client onboarding records, transaction logs, AML alerts) is consistent and readily accessible to both regulators. The MOU also opens the door for coordinated enforcement, meaning that a violation identified by the AMF could quickly become a SEC matter, and vice‑versa.

**IPO Modernization & Crowdfunding Discussion** – The advisory committee’s upcoming meeting could lead to rule changes that lower the cost and complexity of going public for small companies. Fintech firms that assist startups with IPO preparation or operate crowdfunding platforms should monitor the outcomes closely. Adjustments to the fundraising cap or prospectus format could expand the pool of issuers using these services, creating new business opportunities—but also new compliance obligations around disclosure, investor suitability, and reporting.

**Cross‑Trading Rule Expansion** – Registered funds (mutual funds, ETFs, closed‑end funds) often trade securities with affiliates to manage liquidity or achieve tax efficiency. The proposed broadened eligibility list could enable more frequent intra‑group trades, improving operational flexibility. However, the rule adds reporting duties and requires firms to prove that each trade is at fair market value and does not harm other shareholders. Fintech platforms that automate fund‑to‑affiliate trading must update their pricing engines and audit trails to meet the new standards.

Overall, the five items intersect around three themes that matter to fintech compliance teams today: **operational flexibility**, **regulatory scrutiny**, and **cross‑border coordination**. Ignoring any of these could expose firms to enforcement risk, while proactive adaptation can turn the changes into competitive advantages.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review Repo Policies** – Verify that any inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo transactions meet the SEC’s exemption criteria (eligible documentation, risk‑management controls, and counterparty qualifications). Update internal repo‑clearing checklists and flag any repos that now fall outside the exemption.

- **Align with FY‑27 Priorities** – Conduct a gap analysis against the five focus areas (IPO modernization, Regulation Crowdfunding, cyber‑risk, AML for digital assets, cross‑trading compliance). Prioritize remediation of any weak controls, especially in cybersecurity incident response and AML transaction monitoring for crypto‑related activities.

- **Prepare for Cross‑Border Data Requests** – Map the data flows that involve U.S. and French investors. Ensure that client onboarding, KYC, and transaction records are stored in a format that can be shared with both the SEC and AMF under the new MOU. Consider a joint‑review protocol with your legal team to handle simultaneous regulator inquiries.
