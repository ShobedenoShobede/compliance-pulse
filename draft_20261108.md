**HEADLINE**  
SEC Clears Path for More Flexible Treasury Repo Deals, Sets 2027 Exam Focus, Expands Cross‑Trading, and Boosts International Adviser Cooperation – What Fintech Compliance Teams Need to Know Today  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate updates on November 8, 2026.  

1. **Exemptive relief for Treasury repo clearing** – The SEC granted limited exemptions from the mandatory central‑clearing rule for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions involving U.S. Treasury securities. The relief applies when the repo is conducted between affiliates of the same broker‑dealer or between a U.S. broker‑dealer and a non‑U.S. counterpart that does not meet the standard clearing requirements.  

2. **FY 2027 examination priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its priorities for the 2027 fiscal year. The list highlights: (a) the implementation of the SEC’s 2026 IPO‑modernization roadmap, (b) oversight of Regulation Crowdfunding platforms, (c) the use of digital assets in securities offerings, (d) cyber‑risk and third‑party vendor management, and (e) compliance with the new cross‑trading rule for registered funds.  

3. **MOU with France’s AMF** – The SEC signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France. The agreement covers joint examinations, data exchanges on disciplinary actions, and coordinated responses to cross‑border misconduct.  

4. **Small Business Advisory Committee meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on October 27, 2026, to discuss two items: (a) further steps to modernize the IPO process for emerging companies, and (b) potential rule changes to expand the use of Regulation Crowdfunding for early‑stage financing.  

5. **Proposed expansion of cross‑trading eligibility** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would allow registered funds to trade a broader set of securities—including certain corporate bonds and municipal securities—with affiliated entities, provided the trades meet transparency and best‑execution standards.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**1. Repo‑clearing exemptions reduce operational friction for fintech platforms that facilitate inter‑affiliate financing.**  
Fintech firms that act as custodians, liquidity providers, or “bridge” lenders often move Treasury securities between related entities to manage cash flows. The new exemptions mean those firms can keep using bilateral repo structures without immediately investing in a central clearing solution. However, the relief is limited to specific affiliate relationships and non‑U.S. counterparties, so firms must verify that each transaction meets the exemption criteria. Failure to do so could trigger a clearing‑requirement violation and associated penalties.  

**2. FY 2027 exam focus signals where regulators will look first.**  
The exam priorities give compliance teams a roadmap for where the SEC will apply its “risk‑based” lens next year. The emphasis on IPO modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding means platforms that help companies raise capital through online portals must be ready for deeper scrutiny of disclosure practices, investor‑qualification checks, and fee structures. The spotlight on digital assets and cyber‑risk also raises the bar for any fintech that tokenizes securities, uses blockchain settlement, or outsources critical functions to cloud providers.  

**3. The SEC‑AMF MOU raises the bar for cross‑border adviser oversight.**  
Fintech firms that register as investment advisers or provide ancillary services to advisers in both the U.S. and France now face a coordinated supervisory environment. The MOU means that disciplinary findings, enforcement actions, or suspicious‑activity reports shared by the AMF can be used by the SEC in its own examinations, and vice‑versa. Companies must therefore ensure that compliance policies, AML/KYC procedures, and client‑onboarding screens are harmonized across jurisdictions to avoid duplicate findings.  

**4. Small‑business advisory discussions foreshadow rule changes that could affect fintech‑driven IPOs and crowdfunding.**  
If the SEC adopts recommendations from the advisory committee, we could see a lower threshold for “qualified” investors, streamlined prospectus requirements, or expanded use of “continuous” offering models. Fintech platforms that already host IPOs or equity‑crowdfunding campaigns should begin mapping their current processes to the possible new standards, especially around investor verification and ongoing reporting.  

**5. Expanded cross‑trading eligibility creates new opportunities—and new compliance checks.**  
Registered funds that trade with affiliates will now be able to include a wider range of securities in those internal transactions. For fintech firms that manage fund‑level data, execute trades, or provide analytics, this means additional data‑capture requirements: the need to document best‑execution evidence, maintain transparent pricing records, and disclose affiliate‑trade activity to investors. The rule also introduces a “fair‑value” safeguard that may require real‑time pricing feeds for securities that previously were exempt.  

Overall, the five announcements intersect on three themes that are directly relevant to fintech compliance teams: (1) **operational flexibility paired with precise eligibility testing**, (2) **heightened regulator focus on digital‑capital‑raising and cyber‑risk**, and (3) **greater cross‑border supervisory coordination**. Ignoring any of these could lead to enforcement risk, while proactive alignment can turn the changes into competitive advantages.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map every repo transaction against the new exemption criteria.** Create a checklist that captures affiliate relationship, counter‑party domicile, and clearing‑requirement status. Flag any repo that falls outside the exemption and develop a plan to route it through a clearinghouse or seek a separate exemption.  

- **Update your examination‑readiness program for FY 2027 priorities.**  
  * Add a dedicated review of IPO‑modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding controls, focusing on disclosure accuracy, investor‑qualification workflows, and fee transparency.  
  * Conduct a cyber‑risk inventory of all third‑party vendors, especially cloud and blockchain services, and verify that incident‑response plans meet SEC expectations.  
  * Document how digital assets are classified, held, and reported under existing securities rules.  

- **Align U.S. and French compliance frameworks.**  
  * Perform a gap analysis between your U.S. adviser registration program and the AMF’s “Autorité des Marchés Financiers” requirements (e.g., reporting timelines, disciplinary‑record sharing).  
  * Design a single, unified KYC/AML workflow that captures the data elements needed by both regulators, and train staff on the dual‑jurisdiction reporting process.
