**HEADLINE**  
SEC Unveils Repo‑Clearing Relief, FY 2027 Exam Priorities, France MOU, IPO‑Crowdfunding Talk, and Expanded Cross‑Trading Rules – Immediate Implications for Fintech Compliance Teams  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements on November 9, 2026.  

1. **Repo‑Clearing Exemption** – The SEC granted limited exemptive relief from the Treasury‑securities central‑clearing mandate for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions. The relief applies when the repo is executed between affiliates of the same broker‑dealer or when a foreign‑based counter‑party is involved, provided the transaction meets the SEC’s safety‑and‑efficiency criteria.  

2. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year priorities. The top focus areas are: (a) compliance with the new Treasury‑clearing rules, (b) oversight of digital‑asset activities, (c) anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and fraud detection in fintech platforms, (d) risk management for “cloud‑based” service providers, and (e) governance of “embedded finance” products.  

3. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC signed a Memorandum of Understanding with France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The MOU formalizes information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the United States and France. It covers supervisory inspections, enforcement actions, and market‑abuse investigations.  

4. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27, 2026, to discuss two topics: modernizing the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) process for emerging companies and potential rule changes to Regulation Crowdfunding. The committee will consider simplifying prospectus requirements and expanding the fundraising caps for “crowdfunded” securities.  

5. **Proposed Expansion of Cross‑Trading Eligibility** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would allow registered funds to trade a broader class of securities—including certain corporate bonds and exchange‑traded funds (ETFs)—with their affiliates, provided the trades meet fair‑price and best‑execution standards.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑Clearing exemption** directly affects fintech firms that provide repo services or that embed repo financing into trading platforms. The exemption reduces the operational burden of moving every repo into a central clearinghouse, but it also creates a compliance “gap” that regulators may scrutinize. Firms must document why a transaction qualifies for the exemption and retain evidence that the safety standards are met. Failure to do so could trigger enforcement actions for “circumventing” the clearing rule.  

**FY 2027 exam priorities** signal where SEC examiners will focus their resources. The emphasis on digital‑asset activities and cloud‑based service providers means that fintechs using blockchain, stablecoins, or third‑party cloud infrastructure should expect deeper questionnaire reviews and on‑site testing. The priority on “embedded finance” means that companies offering banking‑as‑a‑service, payment‑as‑a‑service, or credit‑as‑a‑service through non‑bank partners must have robust governance, risk, and AML controls in place.  

**The SEC‑AMF MOU** expands the regulator’s view of cross‑border advisory relationships. Fintech platforms that market U.S. advisory services to French investors—or vice‑versa—will now be subject to coordinated supervisory oversight. Information exchanged under the MOU could surface compliance gaps that were previously invisible to a single regulator, increasing the risk of double‑penalties if deficiencies are not addressed.  

**IPO modernization and crowdfunding discussion** could reshape the fundraising landscape for fintech start‑ups. If the SEC adopts a simpler prospectus model or raises the crowdfunding cap, more early‑stage firms may opt for public offerings rather than private rounds. That shift will increase the number of fintechs that must file registration statements, maintain ongoing reporting, and adhere to stricter disclosure standards.  

**Cross‑trading rule expansion** opens new trading opportunities for registered funds that are managed or owned by fintech platforms. While the broader eligibility may improve liquidity and reduce transaction costs, it also raises compliance obligations: firms must ensure that affiliate trades are executed at fair market value, that they are disclosed properly in fund reports, and that they do not create conflicts of interest. The proposed rule will likely be accompanied by detailed record‑keeping and monitoring expectations.  

Overall, the five announcements converge on a single theme: the SEC is tightening oversight of fintech activities that straddle traditional securities markets, digital assets, and cross‑border services. Compliance teams that treat each update in isolation risk missing the broader operational and governance implications.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Map exemption eligibility** – Review all repo agreements in your platform. Identify which qualify for the inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. exemption, and create a checklist (counter‑party relationship, transaction size, risk controls) to document the basis for relief.  

- **Update exam‑readiness checklists** – Align your internal audit schedule with the FY 2027 priorities. Add specific tests for: (i) digital‑asset custody and AML controls, (ii) cloud‑service provider contracts and security assessments, and (iii) governance of embedded‑finance products.  

- **Assess cross‑border advisory exposure** – Inventory any advisory or investment‑service relationships with French entities. Verify that your AML, KYC, and marketing practices meet both SEC and AMF expectations, and prepare to share relevant supervisory data if requested.
