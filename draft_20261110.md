**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s 2026 Moves Shake Up Repo Clearing, Examination Focus, Cross‑Trading Rules, and Small‑Biz Funding – What Fintech Compliance Teams Must Address Right Now  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate items today that touch on core activities for many fintech firms, broker‑dealers, and investment‑service platforms.  

1. **Repo‑Clearing Exemption** – The SEC granted exemptive relief from the mandatory central‑clearing requirement for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions involving Treasury securities. The relief is limited to transactions that meet specific criteria, such as being between affiliates of the same ultimate parent or involving foreign counterparties that are not subject to U.S. clearing mandates.  

2. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its fiscal‑year‑2027 priorities. The list highlights: (a) the effectiveness of internal controls around market‑making and liquidity provision, (b) the use of digital‑asset platforms and related risk‑management practices, (c) compliance with the SEC’s anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and cybersecurity expectations, and (d) the quality of disclosures for complex securities and structured products.  

3. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a memorandum of understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers joint investigations, data‑exchange protocols, and coordinated supervisory actions.  

4. **Small‑Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will meet on Oct. 27 to discuss two topics: modernizing the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) process for emerging companies and potential rule changes to the Regulation Crowdfunding framework. The committee will hear from industry participants about simplifying filing requirements and expanding investor access.  

5. **Cross‑Trading Rule Proposal** – The SEC proposed amendments to the Investment Company Act’s cross‑trading rule. The changes would broaden the types of securities that registered funds may trade with affiliates, provided the transactions meet new transparency and pricing safeguards. The proposal also calls for enhanced reporting to the SEC and to fund shareholders.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Repo‑Clearing exemption reshapes treasury‑repo workflows**  
Fintech platforms that facilitate repo financing for dealers or hedge funds often route Treasury repo trades through central clearing houses to meet regulatory expectations. The new exemption means that, for qualifying inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo deals, firms can keep those trades out of the clearing pipeline. While this can lower operational costs, it also re‑introduces the need for robust internal clearing‑risk controls, margin monitoring, and settlement oversight that the SEC originally sought to eliminate with the clearing mandate. Failure to apply the same rigor could expose firms to settlement failures or regulatory scrutiny.  

**Examination priorities signal where examiners will look first**  
The FY 2027 focus areas are a clear roadmap for examiners. Fintech firms that run digital‑asset exchanges, provide liquidity‑as‑a‑service, or host complex structured products should expect deeper questions about their risk‑management frameworks, cybersecurity posture, and AML programs. The emphasis on “effective internal controls” means that documentation, testing, and independent review of control environments must be up‑to‑date. Companies that have not yet mapped their digital‑asset processes to the SEC’s expectations risk receiving “significant deficiencies” findings.  

**Cross‑border MOU raises the bar for multinational compliance**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU creates a formal channel for the two regulators to share information on U.S.‑France investment advisers and service providers. For fintech firms that serve U.S. investors from a French entity—or vice‑versa—this means that any compliance breach in one jurisdiction can quickly become visible to the other regulator. The MOU also encourages coordinated enforcement, so a single violation could trigger actions in both markets. Firms must therefore align their policies, reporting, and supervisory structures across the Atlantic to avoid duplicated penalties.  

**Small‑biz funding discussions could alter capital‑raising rules**  
If the advisory committee’s recommendations lead to changes in IPO modernization or Regulation Crowdfunding, fintech platforms that help startups raise capital could see new filing formats, lower disclosure thresholds, or expanded investor eligibility. Conversely, a tightening of crowdfunding limits could reduce the pool of retail investors that platforms rely on. Staying ahead of the committee’s output will help firms advise clients on the most efficient path to market and adjust their technology stacks accordingly.  

**Cross‑trading rule expansion affects fund‑of‑funds and wrap‑around services**  
Many fintech providers act as administrators or custodians for registered mutual funds and ETFs. The proposed rule would let those funds trade a broader set of securities with affiliates, but only if they meet stricter pricing and reporting standards. This creates both an opportunity—more flexibility for intra‑group rebalancing—and a compliance burden: firms must capture detailed trade‑level data, perform independent pricing checks, and file additional disclosures. Missing any of these steps could result in enforcement actions for “improper cross‑trading.”

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review repo‑clearing exemptions** – Identify any inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. Treasury repo trades your firm processes. Confirm they meet the SEC’s exemption criteria and document the rationale. If they do not, keep the trades in the central‑clearing workflow or adjust the transaction structure.  

- **Map FY 2027 exam focus to your controls** – Conduct a rapid gap analysis against the four priority areas (market‑making controls, digital‑asset risk, AML/cybersecurity, complex‑security disclosures). Prioritize any missing policies, testing procedures, or reporting mechanisms and schedule remediation within the next 60 days.  

- **Align cross‑border compliance programs** – For any entity operating in both the U.S. and France, synchronize KYC, AML, and reporting policies. Design a single data‑sharing protocol that satisfies both the SEC and AMF, and appoint a joint‑responsibility officer to oversee cross‑jurisdictional issues.
