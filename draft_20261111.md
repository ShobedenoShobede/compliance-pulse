**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s FY 2026 enforcement roundup, new repo‑clearing exemption, FY 2027 exam focus, U.S.–France adviser MOU, and Small‑Biz advisory meeting signal tighter oversight for fintech firms handling securities, clearing, and crowdfunding.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements that together reshape the regulatory landscape for fintech companies that deal with securities issuance, clearing, and advisory services.

1. **FY 2026 Enforcement Results** – The SEC’s Division of Enforcement published its annual results for the fiscal year that ended September 30, 2026. The report details the number of investigations opened, civil actions filed, and penalties assessed. Highlights include a 12 % rise in actions related to misleading disclosures in digital‑asset offerings and a sharp increase in enforcement against firms that failed to implement adequate anti‑money‑laundering (AML) controls in electronic trading platforms.

2. **Exemptive Relief for Certain Repo Transactions** – The SEC granted an exemption from the Treasury‑securities central‑clearing mandate for specific inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) trades. The relief is limited to broker‑dealers and other market participants that can demonstrate a “reasonable basis” that the exemption will not undermine the safety and soundness of the Treasury market. The exemption is effective immediately and will be reviewed annually.

3. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations outlined its priorities for the upcoming fiscal year. Key focus areas are: (a) compliance with the new “digital‑asset securities” guidance, (b) oversight of crowdfunding platforms under Regulation Crowdfunding, (c) evaluation of AML and cybersecurity programs, and (d) assessment of firms’ transition to mandatory central clearing of Treasury securities.

4. **U.S.–France Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and the French Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across both jurisdictions. The MOU covers cross‑border supervisory cooperation, joint investigations, and coordinated enforcement actions.

5. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27, 2026, to discuss “IPO modernization” and potential updates to Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying the registration process for emerging growth companies and expanding the use of “direct listings” for fintech startups.

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement trends signal heightened scrutiny of fintech disclosures and AML controls**  
The FY 2026 enforcement data shows regulators are paying close attention to how fintech firms present digital‑asset offerings and manage AML risks. A 12 % jump in actions tied to misleading digital‑asset disclosures means compliance teams must revisit marketing language, token‑sale prospectuses, and any public statements that could be interpreted as securities offers. Similarly, the rise in AML‑related penalties underscores that generic, “one‑size‑fits‑all” AML programs are no longer sufficient. Fintech platforms must demonstrate a risk‑based approach that reflects the speed, anonymity, and cross‑border nature of digital transactions.

**Repo‑clearing exemption offers short‑term flexibility but introduces new oversight responsibilities**  
The new exemptive relief lets certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repo trades avoid the mandatory Treasury‑securities central‑clearing requirement. While this eases operational burdens for firms that rely on internal repo financing, it also creates a compliance checkpoint: firms must document the “reasonable basis” for the exemption and be prepared for annual SEC review. Failure to maintain adequate risk controls could trigger a revocation of the exemption and expose the firm to enforcement.

**FY 2027 exam priorities map the regulatory road ahead**  
The examination priorities act as a roadmap for where the SEC will focus its resources. Fintech companies that issue digital‑asset securities, run crowdfunding portals, or are in the process of moving Treasury trades to a clearinghouse should expect deeper examinations. The emphasis on cybersecurity and AML means that any gaps in incident‑response plans, penetration‑testing regimes, or transaction‑monitoring systems could become focal points of an exam. Early self‑assessment against these themes can reduce the likelihood of surprise findings.

**Cross‑border MOU raises the stakes for multinational adviser relationships**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU means that French regulators now have a direct line to the SEC for information on U.S.‑registered advisers operating in France, and vice‑versa. Fintech firms that provide advisory or investment‑service functions to clients in both markets must align their compliance programs with the stricter of the two regimes. Data‑privacy, record‑keeping, and reporting obligations may now be subject to coordinated enforcement, increasing the risk of double penalties if gaps exist.

**Crowdfunding and IPO modernization discussions foreshadow regulatory tweaks**  
The upcoming Small Business Advisory Committee meeting signals that the SEC is still evaluating ways to make capital formation easier for fintech startups. Potential changes to Regulation Crowdfunding—such as higher fundraising caps or relaxed investor‑qualification rules—could open new avenues for fintech firms but also bring new compliance requirements, especially around investor disclosures and ongoing reporting. Keeping an eye on the committee’s recommendations will help firms anticipate rule changes before they become mandatory.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Audit digital‑asset disclosures** – Conduct a rapid review of all marketing materials, whitepapers, and token‑sale prospectuses. Verify that every claim is supported by a prospectus‑level filing or a clear exemption, and update language to avoid “investment‑opportunity” phrasing that could be construed as a securities offer.

- **Strengthen AML risk assessments** – Map the end‑to‑end flow of customer funds across all platforms, especially those handling crypto or cross‑border payments. Implement transaction‑monitoring rules that reflect the higher risk of rapid, high‑value movements, and document the risk‑based methodology for internal and regulator review.

- **Document repo‑exemption justification** – If your firm relies on the new repo exemption, prepare a concise “reasonable basis” memo that outlines the trade structure, risk controls, and how the exemption does not compromise Treasury market integrity. Store the memo alongside your clearing‑policy documentation for the upcoming annual SEC review.
