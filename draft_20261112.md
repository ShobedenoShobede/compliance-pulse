**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s 2026 enforcement results, new repo‑clearing exemption, FY 2027 exam focus, France‑US adviser MOU, and Small‑Biz advisory meeting signal tighter oversight and new opportunities for fintech firms  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements that together shape the regulatory landscape for fintech companies in 2026‑27.  

1. **Enforcement Annual Results** – The SEC’s Division of Enforcement published its FY 2026 outcomes, covering the period that ended on September 30, 2026. The report details the number of actions taken, total monetary penalties, and the types of violations that attracted the most attention. While the full numbers are still being digested, the agency emphasized continued focus on protecting investors, ensuring market integrity, and deterring fraud.  

2. **Exemptive Relief for Certain Repo Transactions** – The SEC granted an exemption that eases Treasury‑clearing requirements for specific inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) trades. Broker‑dealers and other participants that conduct repo transactions across affiliate entities or with foreign counterparties can now operate without meeting the standard central‑clearing mandates, provided they satisfy the conditions outlined in the relief order.  

3. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations released its priorities for the upcoming fiscal year. The list highlights four main focus areas: (a) digital‑asset platforms and related securities offerings, (b) fintech firms that provide “bank‑like” services without a banking charter, (c) market‑structure changes tied to new clearing and settlement models, and (d) the effectiveness of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) and know‑your‑customer (KYC) programs in technology‑driven environments.  

4. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a Memorandum of Understanding aimed at improving cooperation on investment advisers and investment‑service providers. The MOU establishes a framework for information sharing, joint supervision, and coordinated action on cross‑border activities that affect U.S. and French investors.  

5. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27, 2026, to discuss two topics: modernizing the initial‑public‑offering (IPO) process and potential changes to Regulation Crowdfunding. The committee will hear from market participants, including fintech platforms that enable small companies to raise capital online, and will consider policy tweaks that could streamline access to public markets.  

---

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement results set the tone for risk** – The FY 2026 enforcement data provides a clear snapshot of what the SEC is targeting. Fintech firms that handle client funds, offer investment advice, or facilitate securities trading should review the highlighted violations—particularly those involving misrepresentations, inadequate disclosures, and weak internal controls. Even if your firm was not directly cited, the trends indicate where regulators will look most closely in the coming year.  

**Repo‑clearing exemption eases operational friction** – For fintechs that run treasury‑repo desks or provide liquidity services, the new exemption removes the need to route certain affiliate or foreign repo trades through a central clearinghouse. This can reduce settlement costs, shorten processing times, and simplify the technology stack. However, the relief is conditional; firms must still maintain robust risk‑management practices and be prepared to demonstrate compliance if the SEC revisits the exemption.  

**FY 2027 exam priorities highlight emerging hotspots** – The four priority themes signal where exam teams will focus their resources. Digital‑asset platforms, especially those issuing tokenized securities or offering crypto‑based investment products, should tighten controls around investor qualification and disclosure. Companies that provide “bank‑like” services—such as payments, lending, or custodial functions—must be ready to explain how they safeguard consumer funds without a banking charter. Finally, any firm that has adopted new clearing or settlement technology should verify that its risk‑monitoring and AML/KYC systems are fully integrated and documented.  

**Cross‑border MOU raises the bar for international advisers** – The SEC‑AMF agreement means that French regulators will have greater visibility into U.S.‑based advisers serving French clients, and vice‑versa. Fintech advisory platforms that operate in both jurisdictions need to align their compliance programs with the expectations of both regulators. Expect more frequent requests for information, joint examinations, and coordinated enforcement actions if gaps are identified.  

**Crowdfunding and IPO modernization could open new product lines** – The advisory committee’s focus on modernizing IPOs and tweaking Regulation Crowdfunding suggests that the SEC may soon adopt rules that make it easier for small companies to raise capital online. Fintech platforms that already support equity crowdfunding should monitor the discussion closely; favorable changes could expand the addressable market, while stricter rules could require additional investor‑qualification steps or reporting obligations.  

Overall, these announcements paint a picture of an SEC that is tightening oversight on high‑growth fintech activities while also offering targeted relief where it sees market friction. Compliance teams must balance the need for stricter controls with the opportunity to leverage regulatory flexibility.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review the FY 2026 enforcement summary** – Pull the key violation categories and compare them against your firm’s policies. Close any gaps in disclosures, record‑keeping, or internal controls that could trigger an enforcement action.  

- **Assess eligibility for the repo‑clearing exemption** – If your firm conducts inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo trades, map the exemption criteria to your current processes. Document the rationale for using the exemption and set up a monitoring routine to ensure ongoing compliance.  

- **Update exam‑readiness checklists** – Incorporate the FY 2027 examination priorities into your internal audit plans. Conduct a focused review of digital‑asset offerings, “bank‑like” service models, and AML/KYC integrations with new clearing technologies.  

- **Align cross‑border compliance with the SEC‑AMF MOU** – For any advisory or investment‑service activities involving French clients, perform a dual‑jurisdiction gap analysis. Harmonize client onboarding, data‑privacy, and reporting procedures to satisfy both U.S. and French regulator expectations.  

- **Prepare for potential crowdfunding rule changes** – Track the Small Business Advisory Committee’s meeting minutes and any forthcoming proposals. Draft a provisional policy for Regulation Crowdfunding that can be quickly adjusted if the SEC adopts new thresholds, investor‑qualification standards, or reporting formats.  

- **Train staff on the new focus areas** – Schedule a short briefing for compliance, legal, and product teams highlighting the enforcement trends, repo exemption, exam priorities
