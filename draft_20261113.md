**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out FY 2026 enforcement results, clears a path for repo‑clearing exemptions, sets FY 2027 exam focus, deepens France‑US adviser cooperation, and signals fresh IPO‑crowdfunding talks – all with immediate compliance knock‑on effects for fintech firms.  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements that together reshape the regulatory landscape for fintech companies, broker‑dealers, and investment‑service providers.  

1. **FY 2026 Enforcement Annual Results** – The SEC’s Division of Enforcement disclosed its year‑end outcomes for the fiscal year that ended September 30, 2026. The report highlights the number of actions taken, the monetary penalties assessed, and the primary misconduct themes that drew enforcement attention. While the full data set is extensive, the key take‑aways are a continued emphasis on protecting investors from fraud, market manipulation, and failures to disclose material information.  

2. **Exemptive Relief for Certain Inter‑Affiliate and Non‑U.S. Repo Transactions** – The SEC granted limited relief from the mandatory central‑clearing requirement for Treasury securities in specific repo trades. The exemption applies to inter‑affiliate arrangements and to repo transactions involving non‑U.S. counterparties that meet defined criteria. The agency framed the move as a way to reduce operational burdens while still encouraging broader adoption of central clearing.  

3. **FY 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations published its priorities for the upcoming fiscal year. The focus areas include: (a) digital assets and crypto‑related activities; (b) environmental, social, and governance (ESG) disclosures; (c) market structure reforms, especially around alternative trading systems; and (d) compliance with the new “modernized” IPO and Regulation Crowdfunding rules that the SEC is piloting.  

4. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing and coordinated oversight of investment advisers and investment‑service providers operating across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers cross‑border supervision, joint examinations, and the exchange of best‑practice guidance.  

5. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting on IPO Modernization & Crowdfunding** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for October 27, 2026, to discuss proposals that would modernize the IPO process and expand the scope of Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying prospectus requirements, adjusting testing‑the‑waters rules, and potentially raising the fundraising caps for crowdfunding portals.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement trends set the compliance bar** – The FY 2026 enforcement results reinforce that the SEC remains vigilant about investor protection. Fintech firms that rely on innovative product designs—such as tokenized securities, robo‑advisory platforms, or AI‑driven trading tools—must double‑check that their disclosures, anti‑fraud controls, and record‑keeping meet the same standards applied to traditional broker‑dealers. The penalties highlighted in the report serve as a reminder that even “new‑fangled” fintech activities are subject to the same enforcement rigor.  

**Repo‑clearing exemption eases operational strain, but adds reporting duties** – The limited relief for inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repo trades means that certain fintechs and market‑making firms can continue using bilateral settlement for Treasury repos, avoiding the cost of integrating with a central clearing counterpart. However, the exemption is conditional. Firms must document eligibility, maintain a clear audit trail, and be prepared to switch to mandatory clearing if the SEC later revokes the relief. Non‑compliance could trigger enforcement action similar to the cases highlighted in the FY 2026 results.  

**Examination priorities flag where the SEC will look next** – The FY 2027 focus on digital assets, ESG, and market‑structure reforms signals that regulators will scrutinize fintech platforms that facilitate crypto trading, provide ESG‑related data, or operate alternative trading venues. Companies should anticipate deeper examination of their AML/KYC controls for crypto, the accuracy of ESG metrics they publish, and the robustness of their order‑routing and best‑execution policies. The upcoming review of IPO and crowdfunding modernization also means that any platform offering capital‑raising services must be ready for revised prospectus and disclosure rules.  

**Cross‑border MOU tightens oversight for U.S.–France advisers** – Fintechs that register as investment advisers or provide ancillary services to U.S. or French clients now face a coordinated supervisory environment. The SEC‑AMF MOU means that deficiencies identified in one jurisdiction can be shared and acted upon by the other. For compliance teams, this translates into a need for harmonized policies, consistent reporting formats, and a single point of contact that can field inquiries from both regulators.  

**Crowdfunding and IPO reforms could open new business lines** – The advisory committee’s upcoming discussion on modernizing IPOs and expanding Regulation Crowdfunding suggests that the SEC may soon relax some of the current restrictions. Fintech platforms that already host equity crowdfunding or plan to launch “direct‑listing” services should begin mapping out how higher fundraising caps, streamlined prospectus templates, or revised testing‑the‑waters rules would affect their product roadmap, risk controls, and investor‑qualification processes.

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review enforcement data for red‑flag patterns.** Pull the FY 2026 enforcement summary into your risk register. Identify any findings that align with your firm’s product lines (e.g., undisclosed material risks, inadequate record‑keeping) and update policies accordingly.  

- **Validate repo‑clearing exemption eligibility.** If your firm engages in inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. Treasury repo trades, confirm that each transaction meets the SEC’s criteria. Document the exemption rationale, retain supporting contracts, and set up a quarterly review to ensure continued compliance.  

- **Map FY 2027 exam focus to internal controls.** Conduct a gap analysis against the four priority areas (digital assets, ESG, market‑structure, IPO/crowdfunding). Prioritize remediation of any weak points—especially AML/KYC for crypto, ESG data verification, and best‑execution monitoring.  

- **Align cross‑border adviser policies.** For any U.S.‑France client relationships, create a joint compliance checklist that satisfies both SEC and AMF reporting requirements. Designate a liaison who can respond to information‑sharing requests from either regulator.
