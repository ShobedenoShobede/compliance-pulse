**HEADLINE**  
SEC rolls out 2026 enforcement results, clears a path for repo‑clearing exemptions, sets FY 2027 exam focus, deepens France‑US adviser cooperation, and tees up IPO‑crowdfunding discussion – all with direct impact on fintech compliance teams.  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**1. 2026 Enforcement Annual Results**  
The SEC released its FY 2026 enforcement statistics. The Division of Enforcement highlighted a continued emphasis on protecting investors and market integrity. The report shows a rise in actions involving digital‑asset offerings, unregistered securities, and violations of anti‑money‑laundering (AML) rules. Notably, the number of cases tied to “technology‑enabled” misconduct – such as platform‑based fraud and improper use of APIs – increased by roughly 15% versus the prior year.  

**2. Exemptive Relief for Certain Repo Transactions**  
The SEC granted an exemption that eases Treasury‑clearing requirements for a narrow class of inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) trades. The relief applies when the repo is executed between affiliates of the same ultimate parent and when the underlying Treasury securities are not required to be centrally cleared under existing rules. The exemption is limited to transactions that meet specific documentation and risk‑management standards the SEC outlined in the release.  

**3. FY 2027 Examination Priorities**  
The Division of Examinations published its FY 2027 priorities. The top themes are: (a) digital‑asset and crypto‑related activities, (b) market‑making and liquidity‑provider conduct, (c) cybersecurity and data‑privacy controls, (d) broker‑dealer AML and sanctions compliance, and (e) the use of artificial‑intelligence tools in trading and client‑onboarding. The SEC also flagged “emerging fintech business models” as an area where examiners will look for gaps between innovative services and existing regulatory expectations.  

**4. SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU**  
The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) signed a Memorandum of Understanding aimed at improving information‑sharing for investment advisers and investment‑service providers that operate across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers joint monitoring of cross‑border adviser registrations, coordinated investigations of misconduct, and the sharing of supervisory findings that could affect U.S. and French market participants.  

**5. Small Business Advisory Committee on IPO Modernization & Crowdfunding**  
The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee scheduled a meeting for Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss proposals that could modernize the IPO process and expand Regulation Crowdfunding. Topics include simplifying prospectus requirements, easing disclosure burdens for “emerging growth companies,” and exploring technology‑driven ways to reach a broader investor base. The committee will also review feedback from fintech platforms that currently facilitate crowdfunding and direct listings.

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Enforcement trends signal higher scrutiny of fintech‑driven misconduct**  
The uptick in enforcement actions tied to digital assets, API misuse, and platform‑based fraud tells compliance teams that the SEC is watching fintech operations closely. Even if your firm does not currently issue digital tokens, the broader lesson is that any technology that automates client onboarding, trade execution, or data sharing must be backed by robust controls. Gaps in AML screening, record‑keeping, or client‑suitability checks can quickly attract examiner attention.  

**Repo‑clearing exemption creates a limited shortcut, but it carries conditions**  
For broker‑dealers that run intra‑company repo programs or that partner with overseas affiliates, the new exemption can reduce the operational cost of mandatory central clearing. However, the relief is conditional on meeting the SEC‑defined documentation, collateral‑valuation, and risk‑monitoring standards. Failure to adhere could result in a reversal of the exemption and exposure to clearing‑related penalties. Fintech firms that automate repo workflows must ensure their systems can capture the required data fields and produce audit‑ready reports.  

**FY 2027 exam priorities map directly onto fintech risk vectors**  
The SEC’s focus on digital‑asset activities, AI‑driven decision‑making, and cybersecurity aligns with the core risk profile of many fintech platforms. Examiners will likely request evidence of: (a) how the firm validates the source and legitimacy of digital‑asset transactions, (b) the governance framework around AI models that affect pricing or client suitability, and (c) incident‑response plans for cyber breaches. Preparing documentation now can shorten the exam timeline and reduce the chance of “significant findings.”  

**Cross‑border adviser cooperation raises the bar for multinational compliance**  
The SEC‑AMF MOU means that advisers operating in both the U.S. and France will face coordinated oversight. Information that the AMF gathers about a firm’s French activities can be shared with the SEC, and vice‑versa. This expands the scope of what regulators can see, making it essential to maintain consistent compliance standards across all jurisdictions, especially around client disclosures, registration status, and anti‑money‑laundering monitoring.  

**IPO‑modernization and crowdfunding discussions could reshape fundraising rules**  
If the SEC adopts any of the committee’s recommendations, fintech platforms that enable Regulation Crowdfunding or assist companies with direct listings may see lighter disclosure requirements and new technology‑friendly filing mechanisms. While this could open growth opportunities, it also means that platforms must be ready to adapt their compliance workflows quickly—updating investor‑qualification checks, adjusting reporting templates, and ensuring that any new digital tools meet the SEC’s data‑integrity standards.

- **Audit your digital‑asset and API controls**

  • Document how your systems detect and prevent unauthorized data flows or fraudulent trade execution.  

- **Assess eligibility for the repo‑clearing exemption**  
  • Verify that any inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo trades meet the SEC’s documentation and risk‑management criteria.  
  • Update your repo‑management platform to capture the required data points (counterparty affiliation, collateral valuation, clearing status).
