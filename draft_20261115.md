**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s FY‑2026 enforcement roundup, new repo‑clearing exemption, FY‑2027 exam focus, US‑France adviser MOU, and Small‑Biz advisory on IPO & crowdfunding – what fintech compliance teams must know today  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The SEC released its FY‑2026 enforcement results, showing a continued emphasis on protecting investors and market integrity. The agency highlighted 112 enforcement actions, including 27 civil penalties and 15 disgorgement orders, many targeting mis‑representations in digital‑asset offerings, insider trading in crypto‑related securities, and failures to supervise fintech platforms. The report also noted a rise in “shadow‑bank” activities tied to non‑bank lenders and peer‑to‑peer lending apps.  

In a separate move, the SEC granted exemptive relief from Treasury‑clearing requirements for certain inter‑affiliate and non‑U.S. repurchase‑agreement (repo) transactions. The relief allows broker‑dealers and clearing participants to keep specific repo trades out of the central‑clearing mandate, provided they meet reporting and risk‑management conditions outlined in the order.  

The agency also announced its FY‑2027 examination priorities, a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with France’s AMF, and a Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee meeting. The examination priorities focus on digital‑asset custody, fintech‑driven market‑making, and the use of artificial‑intelligence tools in compliance. The SEC‑AMF MOU expands information‑sharing on investment advisers and service providers operating across the U.S. and France. Finally, the advisory committee will discuss modernizing IPO processes and potential regulatory changes to Regulation Crowdfunding, signaling possible rule tweaks that could affect fintech platforms facilitating equity crowdfunding.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

For fintech firms that issue, trade, or service digital assets, the enforcement data is a clear warning sign. The spike in actions against misleading token disclosures and inadequate supervisory controls means regulators expect robust, documented procedures for any offering that could be deemed a security. Even firms that do not currently handle securities must assess whether their products or services could fall under the SEC’s broadened definition of “investment contract.”  

The repo‑clearing exemption is directly relevant to fintechs that provide liquidity or financing to broker‑dealers, especially those operating cross‑border or using affiliate structures. By allowing certain repo trades to remain outside the mandatory clearing pipeline, the SEC is easing operational burdens, but it also imposes reporting and risk‑management obligations. Companies must verify that their repo activities meet the exemption’s criteria or risk falling back into the full clearing regime, which could increase capital costs and affect settlement timelines.  

The FY‑2027 exam focus on digital‑asset custody, AI‑driven compliance, and shadow‑banking models means that fintechs should expect deeper scrutiny in these areas. Firms that rely on third‑party custodians, use machine‑learning for transaction monitoring, or offer lending services through platform‑based credit models need to ensure their policies, data‑governance, and oversight structures can withstand a formal SEC examination.  

The new SEC‑AMF MOU expands the regulator’s reach into cross‑border adviser relationships. Fintech platforms that onboard French‑based investment advisers, or that provide advisory services to French investors, must now be prepared for coordinated supervisory reviews. Sharing of information between the two agencies could surface compliance gaps that were previously invisible to a single regulator.  

Finally, the upcoming advisory committee meeting on IPO modernization and Regulation Crowdfunding hints at forthcoming rule changes. Fintech marketplaces that enable private‑company equity offerings or that host crowdfunding campaigns should monitor the discussion closely. Potential adjustments—such as higher fundraising caps, streamlined disclosure templates, or expanded eligibility for “qualified investors”—could open new business opportunities but will also require updates to onboarding, KYC, and ongoing reporting processes.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Review all token and digital‑asset offerings** for any language that could be interpreted as a security; update disclosure templates and ensure a documented supervisory review before launch.  
- **Map your repo transactions** against the SEC’s exemption criteria; confirm that any inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo trades meet reporting and risk‑management thresholds, or prepare to transition them to central clearing.  
- **Strengthen AI‑based monitoring controls** by documenting model inputs, validation procedures, and human oversight; conduct a readiness check against the SEC’s upcoming exam
