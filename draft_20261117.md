**HEADLINE**  
SEC’s 2026 enforcement roundup, new repo‑clearing exemption, FY‑27 exam focus, cross‑border MOU and Small‑Biz advisory meeting signal tighter oversight for fintechs, broker‑dealers and investment‑service platforms.  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission released five separate announcements this week.  

1. **Enforcement Annual Results FY‑2026** – The SEC’s Division of Enforcement published its year‑end statistics, highlighting a 12 % rise in civil actions and a 9 % increase in monetary penalties compared with FY‑2025. The report stresses continued focus on market manipulation, insider trading, and violations involving digital assets and fintech‑enabled trading platforms.  

2. **Exemptive Relief for Inter‑Affiliate and Non‑U.S. Repo Transactions** – The SEC granted a limited exemption from the mandatory central‑clearing rule for Treasury repo trades that occur between affiliated entities or involve non‑U.S. counterparties. The relief is intended to ease the transition to mandatory clearing while preserving liquidity in the repo market. The exemption applies only to transactions that meet specific documentation and risk‑management criteria.  

3. **Fiscal Year 2027 Examination Priorities** – The Division of Examinations outlined its top priorities for the coming year. Key focus areas include: (a) compliance with the new “digital asset” reporting rules, (b) anti‑money‑laundering (AML) controls for crypto‑related activities, (c) supervision of “fintech‑as‑a‑service” models, and (d) oversight of broker‑dealer partnerships with non‑bank fintech firms.  

4. **SEC‑AMF Supervisory MOU** – The SEC and France’s Autorité des Marchés Financiers signed a Memorandum of Understanding to improve information‑sharing on investment advisers and investment‑service providers operating across the two jurisdictions. The MOU covers joint monitoring of cross‑border offerings, coordinated examinations, and a streamlined process for handling complaints.  

5. **Small Business Advisory Committee Meeting on IPO Modernization & Regulation Crowdfunding** – The SEC’s Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee will convene on Oct. 27, 2026, to discuss potential reforms to the IPO process and to evaluate updates to Regulation Crowdfunding. The agenda includes proposals to simplify disclosure, expand investor eligibility, and adjust testing‑the‑waters rules for fintech platforms that facilitate private‑placement offerings.  

---  

### WHY IT MATTERS  

**Heightened Enforcement Pressure** – The FY‑2026 enforcement data makes it clear that the SEC is not easing its pursuit of violations, especially where technology amplifies market impact. Fintech firms that provide trading interfaces, robo‑advice, or digital‑asset custody must expect more frequent inquiries, higher penalties for missteps, and tighter scrutiny of algorithmic trading logs.  

**Repo‑Clearing Exemption Is a Double‑Edged Sword** – While the exemption eases the operational burden for broker‑dealers with affiliate or offshore repo relationships, it also creates a compliance carve‑out that must be documented meticulously. Failure to meet the exemption’s documentation standards could trigger a violation of the broader clearing mandate, exposing firms to enforcement action and reputational risk.  

**FY‑27 Examination Priorities Target Fintech Core Functions** – The upcoming examination focus aligns directly with the services many fintechs now provide: digital‑asset custody, AML screening for crypto, and “as‑a‑service” platforms that outsource brokerage or advisory functions. Companies that have not yet mapped these risk areas to their internal controls will likely be flagged in the next wave of examinations.  

**Cross‑Border MOU Raises International Coordination** – The SEC‑AMF MOU means that French regulators will have a direct line to the SEC on matters involving U.S. investors in French‑registered products and vice‑versa. Fintech platforms that market securities or advisory services across the Atlantic must be prepared for joint examinations and shared enforcement findings.  

**Potential Changes to IPO and Crowdfunding Rules Could Alter Business Models** – Any modernization of IPO filing requirements or adjustments to Regulation Crowdfunding will affect fintechs that act as “marketplaces” for private‑company fundraising. Simplified disclosure could lower entry barriers, but expanded investor eligibility may increase the compliance load for KYC/AML and suitability assessments.  

---  

### WHAT TO DO  

- **Update Enforcement‑Risk Registers** – Incorporate the FY‑2026 enforcement trends into your risk‑assessment tools. Flag any processes that involve digital‑asset trading, algorithmic execution, or high‑frequency order routing for a detailed review.  

- **Document Repo‑Exemption Eligibility** – If your firm engages in inter‑affiliate or non‑U.S. repo trades, create a standing checklist that captures the required documentation (trade confirmations, affiliate relationship disclosures, risk‑management policies). Store the evidence in a centralized repository for easy retrieval during a regulator audit.  

- **Map FY‑27 Examination Priorities to Existing Controls** – Conduct a gap analysis against the four priority areas. Verify that your AML program covers crypto transaction monitoring, that digital‑asset reporting aligns with the SEC’s new Form ATS requirements, and that any “fintech‑as‑a‑service” contracts include clear compliance clauses.  

- **Prepare for Cross‑Border Supervisory Requests** – Review all French‑related offerings or advisory relationships. Ensure that data‑sharing agreements, client disclosures, and complaint‑handling procedures meet both SEC and AMF expectations. Designate a point‑person for any joint examination requests.  

- **Assess Impact of Potential IPO/Crowdfunding Reforms** – Model how simplified IPO disclosures or expanded crowdfunding investor caps would affect your platform’s onboarding, suitability checks, and ongoing reporting. If the models show material cost or risk changes, begin drafting internal policy updates now so you can implement them quickly if the reforms are adopted.  

- **Train Front‑Line Teams on New Enforcement Signals** – Hold a brief, targeted training session for sales, product, and operations staff. Emphasize the heightened focus on digital assets, the need for precise repo documentation, and the importance of accurate AML screening for cross‑border transactions.  

- **Set Up a Monitoring Routine for Regulatory Updates** – Assign a compliance analyst to track SEC press releases, especially the SEC‑AMF MOU and upcoming advisory committee outcomes. A weekly 15‑minute briefing will keep the team ahead of any rule‑making that could affect product roadmaps.  

By taking these steps now, fintech compliance teams can stay ahead of the SEC’s intensified enforcement posture, avoid pitfalls around the repo‑clearing exemption, and position themselves for smoother cross‑border operations and upcoming market‑structure reforms.
